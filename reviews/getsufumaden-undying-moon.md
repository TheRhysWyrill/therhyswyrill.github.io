Thirty-five years after its Famicom original sent the first Getsu Fuma into hell, GetsuFumaDen Undying Moon revives the property as a 2.5D roguelike action platformer steeped in ukiyo-e Yōkai horror. The premise sends the warrior below to quell a cataclysm and avenge his brothers, with mythic Japanese horror supplying tone throughout. Enemies and bosses each carry lore entries worth reading, and environmental storytelling lands well, with background giants foreshadowing the encounters ahead. The aesthetic earns praise early and never stops earning it, an art direction nothing else on the platform quite matches. Layered systems build quickly on a familiar delve-and-die foundation.

Demonization is the standout mechanic, powering the warrior up for playing flawlessly and rewarding untouched health directly. The better the play, the better the performance, turning skill expression into a resource rather than a bragging right. It ties directly into the roguelike loop, giving every dodge and parry a reason beyond survival. Few mechanics communicate the intended playstyle this legibly, and the meter's generosity makes the flawless stretches feel earned rather than demanded.

Crafting runs on fragments plus diagrams, a smithing system praised for its simplicity and the absence of obscure material hunting. Weapon refinement and the initial-weapon-rack customisation deepen the loop without complicating it, and permanent mastery skills stack across runs. Health upgrades vastly outvalue weapon upgrades, a tactical realisation that reshapes the entire build economy, since weapon ranks deliver marginal gains while a health rank jump transforms survivability. Gauntlets emerge as an unexpected favourite, a weapon type nobody expects to top the hierarchy yet one that earns the slot repeatedly.

Status effects are potent and fun, with poison, bleed and freeze shredding enemies at several damage per second for extended durations, though the triggers resist full understanding and the effects run close to overpowered. Area size runs uneven, with early zones flying by while the Frozen Graveyard sprawls enormous, distorting run pacing between sprints and treks. The branching route forces a choice of one area from two, ending any plan to see everything in a single run and pushing completion across many. Difficulty tiers change enemy availability and coin and material rates, with lower tiers serving as material farming.

Skill descriptions are so vague that large stretches pass without understanding what upgrades actually do, the single largest gripe against the systems. Not every weapon type has a real moveset, with big weapons and chains reduced to a single swing, undermining the variety the racks promise. R1 does double duty as interact and weapon-switch, causing accidental loadout swaps at the worst moments. One fatal-error crash claimed a session two hours in, the campaign's only stability blot, though a memorable one.

The estate bank anchors the risk and reward economy: bank the haul or gamble it on a deeper delve, and greed costs everything. Learning the banking discipline after a loss is the loop's sharpest lesson, and deliberately gambling runs to maximise materials is where the design sings. The bestiary rewards completionists with lore entries for every Yōkai encountered. The Rando duel provides the campaign's most memorable structured fight.

Overall, GetsuFumaDen Undying Moon is a stylish, systems-rich roguelike sequel whose demonization skill-expression, fragment smithing and ukiyo-e Yōkai aesthetic deliver a deeply enjoyable loop, held back by vague skill text, single-swing heavy weapons and uneven area sprawl. The friction never dents the fun, and the revival honours its 1987 source while feeling modern. A rare sequel that justifies a thirty-five-year wait.
