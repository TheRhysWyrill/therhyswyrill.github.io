Myst III: Exile is a point-and-click adventure from Presto Studios, following a stolen linking book into the four Ages written by Atrus. The complete run covers J'nanin and the three Ages of Voltaic, Amateria and Edanna, closing with the Releeshahn conclusion.

The four Ages each speak a different puzzle language, from J'nanin's colour-beam alignment to Voltaic's power-and-water machinery. Amateria's counterweight physics and ball-launching mechanics earn the strongest visual praise, while Edanna's maze-like nature age guides with sound and light.

The revenge narrative unfolds through Saavedro's messages and paintings, with the memory-loss motif tracking his fractured state across the Ages. The text-heavy journals carry much of the story, with the writing remaining legible throughout and the music turning sinister in places.

The Ages can be entered and left freely, a clear improvement over the first Myst's locked progression. The point-and-click node movement warps between viewpoints, though the movement itself feels jerky next to the modern remakes.

The 2001-era visuals show their age, with the small default window fixed by the bundled widescreen mod. The legible text is a clear step up from the cramped journals of the previous entry, though the reading load stays heavy throughout.

The vibration puzzle proves the stickiest moment, with its dial symbols rather than sounds hiding the solution. The maze-like nature age is easy to get lost in, and the colour puzzles offer no help for colour-blind players.

Overall, the puzzle variety, the free-return structure and the revenge narrative make this a fun, creative entry in the series. The vibration puzzle and the jerky movement are the main caveats.
