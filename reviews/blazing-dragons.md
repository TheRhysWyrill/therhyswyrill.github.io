Dragons protagonist Flicker dreams of becoming a knight to save Princess Flame from an unwanted marriage in this medieval fantasy where reptiles are heroes and humans are the villains. The premise immediately subverts familiar fairy tale expectations, establishing a comedic tone that persists across every interaction. Dialogue and puzzle encounters maintain this irreverent atmosphere throughout the adventure.

Comedy drives the experience from the opening dialogue, with witty writing that parodies point-and-click conventions while maintaining an affectionate tone toward the genre. Voice acting elevates every scene, delivering performances that land jokes with timing and energy well beyond what the 1996 release date suggests. Characters like the bumbling Knights of the Square Table and the villainous Sir George carry distinct personalities through vocal delivery alone.

Point-and-click mechanics translate smoothly to the PlayStation controller, with cursor movement and inventory management feeling intuitive despite the platform's limitations. A semi-enchanted map tracks character locations, reducing the backtracking that plagues many adventure games of the era. Puzzles demand creative item combinations, from using stilts to cross gaps to deploying popcorn as an unlikely weapon against a dragon.

The Cave of Dilemma sequence introduces environmental challenges that break up the conversation-driven pacing, demanding spatial awareness alongside inventory logic. A catapult mini-game adds mechanical variety without disrupting the adventure's flow. These moments reward observation and lateral thinking, though some puzzle solutions require collecting seemingly random items that prove essential later.

Subtitles occasionally cut off mid-sentence, creating moments of confusion during dialogue-heavy sequences. The L1/R1 button mapping for switching interaction modes proves easy to mix up during rapid exchanges. These issues remain minor inconveniences rather than significant obstacles.

Overall, the inverted fantasy premise and exceptional voice acting deliver a comedy-driven adventure that maintains momentum through sharp writing and memorable characters, despite occasional subtitle glitches and an unintuitive control scheme.
