Call of Duty: Ghosts defines its post-apocalyptic narrative through a gritty, defensive struggle following a catastrophic orbital weapon strike. Rather than relying on standard corridor skirmishes, the campaign prioritises creative set pieces that successfully break away from established franchise formulas with surprising ambition.

Underwater sabotage missions and high-stakes skyscraper infiltrations elevate the experience by forcing players to master specialised tactical equipment like ascenders and unique rifles. These inventive scenarios demonstrate a clear willingness to experiment with environmental constraints that distinguish this entry from its more grounded and linear series predecessors.

Directing a combat dog provides a refreshing layer of strategy during intense engagements, allowing for precise reconnaissance and tactical attacks against entrenched adversaries. Utilising this canine companion creates a distinct squad-based rhythm that feels genuinely impactful amidst the backdrop of a desperate, resource-starved war.

Visuals are unfortunately hampered by an unnaturally low camera perspective, making the protagonist feel physically smaller than the surrounding non-player characters. This strange height discrepancy creates a jarring sense of scale that persists throughout the journey, occasionally hindering the immersion during close-quarters shootouts or heavy stealth-oriented infiltration segments.

Engine limitations result in erratic frame pacing and problematic texture streaming that remain visible despite attempts to stabilise the environment across various software configurations. Asset loading failures frequently manifest as distracting visual glitches, serving as a constant reminder that the underlying technical foundation struggles to support such ambitious scale.

Combat encounters demand careful movement as enemy soldiers demonstrate an aggressive reliance on grenade usage that can quickly overwhelm unprepared players. Mastering these high-intensity firefights requires constant vigilance, especially when transitions into remote sniper sequences force players into predefined roles instead of allowing for a more natural shift.

Overall, this surprisingly engaging campaign offers several inventive highlights that deserve far more recognition than its controversial reputation might suggest. Anyone prepared to overlook the persistent technical flaws and embrace the diverse level design will discover a solid, highly enjoyable shooter that manages to carve out a unique identity.
