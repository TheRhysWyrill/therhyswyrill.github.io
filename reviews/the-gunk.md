A scrapping duo lands on a lush alien planetoid chasing an energy signal and finds the whole ecosystem being smothered by a corruptive black substance called the Gunk. The premise sets up a light sci-fi buddy story with a prosthetic-armed protagonist, an alien-ruins mystery involving statues of a figure called the Gardener, untranslated inscriptions and pods holding a sleeping population, and a clean environmental fable about restoring a world. The tone is good-natured throughout, closer to a relaxed collectathon adventure than a hostile survival story.

The vacuum-glove core loop makes environmental cleanup the entire fantasy. Scanning flora, sucking up materials, cleaning Gunk to restore a region's colour and reveal hidden paths, and building bombs, mushroom staircases and tongue-bridges as traversal puzzles gives the planet a tactile, restorative rhythm. Few adventures make the act of cleaning itself the traversal, combat and reward system all at once, and the loop stays satisfying from start to finish.

The colour restoration gives the exploration its constant visual payoff. Bright, healthy regions contrast sharply with the grim, corrupted patches, and the closer the story moves toward the source, the worse the landscape looks. The colourful, pretty art direction is the game's defining quality, from the fungal jungle's early vistas to the late Garden, and the credits sequence carries the same visual craft through to the end. Every cleaned patch feels earned, and the contrast keeps the restoration loop legible.

Upgrades are the weakest progression link. Materials gate improvements to sprint, vacuum speed and health, but they are only craftable at camp beacons and are so situational that they mostly serve completionism rather than necessity. Creature spawns are finite, which keeps the world tidy but also means material hunting has a ceiling, and the light combat, where Gunk critters are picked up and thrown like explosive footballs and spitters are stunned when vacuumed, never demands the stat boosts. Traversal toys outshine the stat upgrades entirely, and a player who ignores the camp crafting altogether loses almost nothing.

Navigation confusion recurs through the caves and Garden chapters. Getting lost in the mid-game mine section, missing obvious paths and doubling back on routes already walked all happen often enough to interrupt the relaxed pace, and the self-directed mockery of retracing ground says more about signposting than about the player. The cave chapter is the low point for orientation, while the open Garden restores momentum once the routes open up again.

Stability is strong. A single bug across the whole campaign, a character stuck on a rock with saving disabled while airborne, is the only technical blemish, and it is a minor one. The Gardener boss closes the story across two phases without escalation into unfairness, and a comedic post-credits exchange gives the ending a light sign-off. Performance stays smooth throughout, with no visual or frame issues to report.

The whole planetoid can be cleared in a brisk handful of hours, and the pacing never stalls long enough for the simple loops to curdle into tedium. Hidden material caches reward poking into corners, and exploration reliably pays off for anyone who detours from the critical path, which redeems an early stretch where the world feels sparse. The scanning rhythm and the restoration payoff sustain the compact shape all the way through.

The alien-ruins mystery provides the narrative spine that the buddy banter alone would not. the Gardener's identity through statues, inscriptions and the sleeping pods gives the exploration a slowly-unfolding purpose, and the environmental stakes stay legible without heavy-handed exposition. The bond between the two scrappers keeps the tone warm, and the three-fingered character designs give the cast a distinct look that suits the stylised world.

Overall, this is a bright, good-natured alien adventure whose vacuum-and-restore loop and constant visual payoff make environmental cleanup genuinely satisfying, with a well-paced alien mystery underneath the buddy banter. Situational upgrades, camp-only crafting, occasional navigation confusion and one small bug are the total cost, and none of it undermines a campaign that stays pleasant from first scan to final cleanup.
