Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III represents a staggering decline in quality, failing to provide the cohesive military spectacle long-time followers of the series demand. This campaign stumbles through a convoluted narrative that treats iconic legacy characters as hollow shells, leaving an impression of design confusion rather than narrative weight.

Visual glitches constantly plague the presentation, ranging from screen-filling squares and strange artifacts to persistent, jarring lighting distortions. Technical instability frequently forces the software to crash, creating a barrier that makes finishing the already uninspired mission roster feel like a strenuous test of one's patience and hardware endurance.

Open combat missions contribute heavily to this exhaustion, as they frequently recycle assets derived from other modes instead of building unique environments. Playing through these poorly implemented encounters reveals awkward mechanics, such as sluggish movement, clumsy equipment deployment, and enemy artificial intelligence that behaves with frustratingly bizarre or inconsistent logic.

Heavily armoured foes stand out as the most tedious element, functioning as sponges that soak up bullets without providing a satisfying tactical challenge. Engaging these durable opponents ruins the pacing of firefights, turning what should be tense, rapid-fire confrontations into repetitive exercises that lack the punchy feedback of earlier entries.

Specific linear missions, such as the initial raid, briefly capture the high-intensity drama that once defined these blockbuster military adventures. Unfortunately, such flashes of brilliance are rare, disappearing quickly as the formula stagnates into a monotonous cycle of detachment from the core narrative goals and repetitive, ill-fitting environmental objectives.

Choice-driven dialogue options appear entirely superficial, failing to influence the outcome or deepen the player's connection to the story. These hollow narrative additions serve only to distance the audience from the plot, reinforcing the perception that the entire product is a disjointed collection of confusing, poorly considered creative decisions.

Overall, this entry functions as a glaring example of how to mismanage a storied franchise through questionable design and extreme technical instability. The experience offers very little beyond morbid curiosity for the rebooted plot, ultimately failing to deliver the thrill or polish expected from a major contemporary shooter.
