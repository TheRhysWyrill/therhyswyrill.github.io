Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III delivers a very strong start with initial cinematic missions like Operation 627, showcasing high intensity and a great level of visual polish. This explosive opening supports satisfying gameplay mechanics, anchoring the experience with great gunplay that feels particularly rewarding when utilizing tactical thermal scopes in low-visibility environments. The campaign benefits from very good voice acting and actor performances, while smooth cinematic transitions seamlessly blend the narrative cutscenes into active gameplay during the London-based segments.

The core gameplay loop thrives on creative mission concepts, offering interesting departures from standard play through the Deep Cover stealth segment and the high-stakes sniper overwatch of the Payload mission. Navigating these combat fields relies on excellent tactical gadgets and environmental utility, allowing you to use snapshot pulses and target explosive hazards or SAM sites with an AC-130 arsenal. You will also notice that character movement feels much quicker and faster paced during the open combat segments compared to traditional linear levels.

However, this premium shooter experience faces severe structural hurdles due to the heavy overuse of Open Combat Missions, which feel completely shoehorned in back-to-back. The campaign suffers from recycled map assets, introducing the deep disappointment of environments that are essentially just Warzone maps repurposed for a paid single-player experience. This friction is compounded by intense technical instability, resulting in a game that locks up and crashes a lot unless you resort to running administrative workarounds.

The user experience introduces additional headaches through a confusing narrative and poor continuity, as the game makes the baffling decision to tie critical lore directly to separate Warzone events. You will also encounter artificial AI behavior, including incredibly spongy armored enemies that ruin the tactical pacing alongside squadmates who act as total bullet magnets. This layout is further restricted by hand-holding puzzle logic that immediately highlights solutions, alongside inconsistent character designs that fail to match the personalities of their original trilogy counterparts.

The narrative tension is frequently undermined by questionable plot armor, showcasing weird survival scenarios like surviving lethal gas exposure in the reactor that completely breaks realism. The visual presentation is also marred by technical bugs during high-intensity weather sequences, introducing distracting visual artifacts and weird distortion like flickering squares across the screen. Despite the solid foundational shooting mechanics, the overall package feels rushed and lacks the depth of a proper standalone entry.

Overall Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III is a structurally flawed, narratively confusing, and technically unstable release that squanders its strong cinematic openings and crisp gunplay on lazy, recycled Warzone assets. While the experience offers quick movement speeds, excellent voice work, and a few creative stealth gadgets, the campaign is heavily dragged down by spongy armored enemies, constant game crashes, and a frustrating reliance on shoehorned open combat maps. Because it sacrifices premium single-player quality for repetitive asset reuse and narrative missteps, this campaign is ultimately not recommended.
