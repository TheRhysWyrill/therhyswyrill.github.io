Greedy Pea returns for a second helping of green-tinted precision platforming across 25 compact stages. The Game Boy-inspired pixel aesthetic and chiptune soundtrack create an immediate retro identity, while the core loop of navigating hazards and collecting coins delivers tight, repeatable challenges. The double jump feels like a meaningful evolution over its predecessor.

Each stage pairs a distinct hazard layout with its own musical track, creating small pockets of personality within the Game Boy green palette. Coins scattered across platforms serve dual purposes: rewarding precise movement and guiding traversal. The boss stages every five levels break up the standard platforming rhythm, offering brief shifts in pacing.

The double jump solves the frustration of falling off ledges that plagued the first game, allowing mid-air corrections that make tricky platforming sequences feel fair rather than punishing. Coin placement rewards exploration and verticality, turning each level into a micro-challenge of routing and timing.

The hitboxes are inconsistent, sometimes forgiving and sometimes baffling under identical circumstances. Later saw blade gauntlets spike the difficulty sharply, creating sections where repeated deaths feel arbitrary rather than skill-based. One particular gauntlet can consume over fifteen minutes of retries before the muscle memory finally clicks.

Several levels appear to be near-duplicates of earlier ones with minimal changes, padding the content without adding variety. The control scheme has an odd quirk where the analogue stick only registers leftward movement, requiring the d-pad for rightward traversal. The CRT filter visual option offers fewer configuration choices than the first game.

Overall, the chiptune soundtrack and double jump mechanic elevate this above its predecessor, delivering a retro platformer with distinct stage identities that persist despite inconsistent hitboxes and lazy level duplication. The coin collection system sustains momentum even when individual levels push difficulty past the point of enjoyment.
