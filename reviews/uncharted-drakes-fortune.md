A coffin recovery off Panama opens a hunt for El Dorado through jungle ruins, a stranded U-boat, a flooded colony and a cursed vault. Quippy banter among a fortune hunter, a debt-ridden mentor and a camera-carrying journalist carries the tone between gunfights. Blockbuster set pieces punctuate the trail at a cinematic clip, with heavy cutscenes binding climbs and firefights together. A late horror-tinged turn shifts the register without warning, trading grounded treasure hunting for mutant dread. Atmosphere stays pulpy throughout, buoyant rather than grim, and momentum survives every mechanical complaint.

Climbing and cover shooting alternate as the core loop, with handholds and chest-high walls trading places across ruins and wrecks. Light puzzles break the rhythm: a lamp-order brazier, scripture-statue facing, bell harmony, symbol dials and a vault number path. Long-range pistols and the M4 answer most firefights, with grenade arcs and explosives covering clusters, while shotguns sit sidelined. Shoulder-switching and mid-fight QTE prompts add texture to the shooting without deepening it. Boat ambushes, a plane crash, a jet-ski run, a jeep chase and a bunker generator sequence supply the blockbuster peaks.

The debt pursuit behind the hunt gives every ruin a reason, with mercenary opposition pressing from the first ambush to the cursed vault. Banter does the heavy lifting, keeping the crew likeable across long climbs and longer shootouts. Heavy cutscene density slows the pace in stretches, trading control for cinema. Mutant horror arrives with no grounding in the treasure hunt before it, a sharp surprise that reframes the back half. Vagueness here is deliberate; the turn lands harder unspoiled.

Boat ambushes open the set-piece account with deck-to-deck firefights on open water. Plane crash wreckage turns survival into vertical climbing through broken fuselage. Jet-ski runs and the jeep chase trade cover for speed, with mounted shooting across waves and dust. Bunker generator rooms plunge the pace into darkness, where a crank sequence stalls momentum in the gloom. Vault number paths and the final mercenary press close the spectacle with puzzles under fire and a last gauntlet run.

Bullet-sponge enemies soak absurd punishment while return fire kills fast, draining firefights of fairness. Basic shooting stays serviceable, favouring disciplined pistol and rifle work over shotgun rushes. Quick deaths punish positioning errors harder than the default easy setting suggests. Mercenary packs and late mutant rushes compound the sponge problem with numbers. Combat never collapses, but balance stays the weakest register throughout.

Climbing and jump-grabbing stay inconsistent, with clean jumps face-planting instead of connecting for no clear reason. Grab detection turns vertical escapes into gambles at the worst moments. Puzzles stay light by design, offering breather beats rather than resistance. The PC recomp build runs start to finish with only negligible hitches, a smooth path outside original hardware. Minor technical friction never interrupts the blockbuster clip.

Set-piece density over mechanical depth defines the identity: a playable blockbuster rather than a systems showcase. Crew banter and cinematic pacing outweigh every mechanical complaint across the campaign. The climb-shoot-puzzle rotation repeats without wearing thin, since new ruins and wrecks refresh the backdrop. Easy difficulty keeps the spectacle accessible while sponge and grab friction persist. The opener earns its place through charm and momentum rather than precision.

Overall, quippy crew banter, blockbuster set pieces and a late horror turn carry a treasure hunt whose shooting stays basic and whose grabs stay unreliable. Sponge enemies and heavy cutscenes blur the finish; pulpy momentum and eerie late variety give the expedition lasting charm.
