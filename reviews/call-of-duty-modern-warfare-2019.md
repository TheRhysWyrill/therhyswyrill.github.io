Multiple characters navigate London and Urzikstan through terrorism, chemical weapons, and moral ambiguity, establishing a tone consistently darker than previous entries. The atmosphere is intense throughout, with content warnings preparing players for sequences that test emotional resilience rather than mechanical skill.

The Clean House night vision mission stands as the campaign's atmospheric peak, with limited visibility and careful movement creating genuine tension. The stealth mechanics demand patience and precision, rewarding methodical play over aggressive engagement through the darkened urban environment.

RC plane missions provide surprisingly creative gameplay variation, breaking the infantry-focused rhythm with remote-controlled aerial attacks. These sequences demonstrate the campaign's willingness to experiment beyond the established formula, adding mechanical diversity that sustains engagement across the mission structure.

Slower, more deliberate movement distinguishes this entry from the franchise's typical pace, creating a more grounded combat experience. The intentional design choice demands tactical positioning rather than reflexive movement, with the mounting mechanic and aim-down-sights reloading reinforcing the deliberate approach.

Unclear objectives occasionally create confusion during exploration sequences, with the player struggling to identify the next objective marker. The campaign's commitment to realism sometimes extends to navigational clarity, where environmental storytelling takes precedence over explicit guidance.

The emotional storytelling delivers genuine weight through morally complex scenarios, with interrogation sequences and civilian encounters that challenge typical shooter conventions. These moments create lasting impact that extends beyond the mechanical experience, establishing a narrative identity distinct from the franchise's action-movie predecessors.

Overall, the night vision missions and emotional depth establish a Call of Duty reboot with genuine atmospheric ambition, though slower movement and unclear objectives prevent the experience from fully realising its potential as a grounded military shooter.
