Contra: Hard Corps reinvents the run and gun formula with branching missions, four selectable fighters and an unusually present plot. The Mega Drive audio is the standout, with the crunchy sound chip and the boss themes singled out as the game's finest feature.

The narrative marks a real departure for the franchise, unfolding through cutscenes and genuine choice. Mission paths diverge around pursuing a mercenary, confronting a doctor and answering a colonel's offer, so separate playthroughs reach genuinely different levels.

Boss encounters lean into absurd creativity, with morphing star-sign forms, a cyberspace stage and laboratory creatures fused together in grotesque combinations. A hoverbike that transforms like a Power Rangers machine and wall-climbing add welcome movement variety between the gunfights.

The spread shot remains the standout weapon, and switching shot types mid-run rewards quick reactions. Weapons are short-lived, however, so any attachment to a favourite loadout rarely survives the next death.

Hitbox readability is the main weakness, since it is rarely clear which hazards can be crossed safely and which cause damage. Several boss attacks feel unavoidable on first sight, with little indication of the correct escape route.

Control layout adds friction, with the jump, weapon switch and shot-type buttons easy to confuse throughout the campaign. Infinite lives soften the sting of the one-hit deaths, but the frequent mistakes still interrupt the flow.

Overall, the soundtrack and the branching structure make this the strongest entry in the series so far. The set-piece creativity and the genuine story mark a clear upgrade over everything that came before it.
