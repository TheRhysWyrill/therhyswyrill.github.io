Contra Hard Corps thrusts a paramilitary unit into a crisis triggered by an alien cell stolen from a research centre. Multiple playable characters, including a cybernetic wolf and a combat robot, offer distinct abilities that encourage replaying through divergent branches. The Mega Drive's sound chip delivers relentless chiptune compositions.

Branching paths split the campaign into separate levels depending on mission targets, rewarding exploration with fresh environments and boss encounters. Combat demands rapid reflexes, with scrolling stages filled with environmental hazards. Vehicle sections on motorcycles and boats break up the foot-based platforming. These sequences maintain shooting intensity while shifting perspective.

Four distinct characters bring different weapon loadouts and movement properties to each run. The robot trades mobility for heavier firepower, while the wolf emphasises agility. Cycling between these options reveals how level design accommodates each character's strengths.

Vehicle sequences inject variety into the core shooting mechanics. Motorcycle chases through city streets demand precise lateral movement while maintaining forward fire, and boat encounters introduce wave-based combat on water. These diversions prevent the formula from growing stale across the branching paths. Each demands different positioning.

Boss encounters escalate in complexity, with late-game fights layering multiple attack patterns simultaneously. Some attacks lack clear telegraphing, making hit detection difficult to parse against sprite density. The soundtrack peaks during these encounters. Late-game bosses demand precise pattern memorisation.

Muted colour palettes contrast sharply with vibrant cybernetic environments, giving each route a distinct visual identity. The difficulty curve remains steep, demanding memorisation of enemy placement and attack timing. Platforming precision becomes critical as enemy density increases. Each branching route presents escalating challenge.

Overall, the chiptune compositions and branching level structure deliver a run-and-gun with substantial replay value. Hit detection inconsistencies and unclear enemy telegraphs create friction that prevents the combat from reaching its full potential.
