Alien Breed: Impact is a top-down shooter in Team17's rebooted series, placing engineer Conrad aboard alien-infested spacecraft where each level asks for objectives to be completed before reaching the exit. Key cards, power restoration and scattered data pads structure the missions, with alien swarms attacking in numbers throughout.

Twin-stick style movement lets the protagonist run and shoot in all directions, and the camera can be rotated in increments. Weapon variety and the option to improve gear keep the shooting loop responsive across the short campaign.

Enemy variety is limited, with the bog-standard big alien and chargers dominating and screamers appearing only near the end. Swarms can overwhelm at points, where the wrong weapon makes a corridor hard to push through.

The sparse autosave proves a genuine kindness, protecting progress without ever feeling like a safety net. The credits arrived without fuss, marking a clean, brief run through the trilogy's opening chapter.

The presentation carries a solid indie sheen, with the horror of the ghost ship setting coming through in the derelict corridors. The story's cliffhanger ending sets up the sequels cleanly.

Nostalgia colours the reception, since the original Xbox 360 release was remembered fondly, and the Steam version reviewed here holds up as a tidy reintroduction. The complaints about swarming and enemy repetition read as minor alongside the brisk pacing.

Overall, shooting design makes Alien Breed: Impact a compact and competent reintroduction to the series, with autosave and tight pacing smoothing its rough edges. The limited enemy roster and occasional swarms keep it from greatness, but not from a recommendation.
