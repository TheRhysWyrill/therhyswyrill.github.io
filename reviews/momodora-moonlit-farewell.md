A village priestess defending her home under a lunar deity's shadow is the premise, delivered through in-world notes and records that catch new players up without demanding series homework. The lore builds to genuinely mythic scale, with the moon-blood creation myth, lunar attunement and the twin figures of Linth and Selin grounding a story that side characters like the archaeologist Meloth help unpack. The tone holds serene-between-violence beautifully: a relaxing score, healing Bell rituals at shrines, and a village full of pettable cats give the whole thing a warmth unusual for the genre.

The pixel art is a dramatic series-wide leap, with sprite scale and animation budget visibly outclassing Reverie Under the Moonlight and everything else before it. It is the first thing that lands and the thing that keeps landing: every area is a backdrop worth lingering in, and the village details repay attention throughout. The series has evolved from its Cave Story-engine roots into its own identity across fifteen years, and this is the definitive expression of that evolution.

Traversal defines the structure: a leaf-melee and bow core loop gated behind sprint, double jump and wall jump unlocks, each one reframing exploration. The late wall jump is the screw-attack moment, unlocking routes and psychology in equal measure; double jump doubles as a safety net that changes how boldly ledges get approached. Once the power curve crests, enemies one-tap and the game feels wonderful. A giant cat retainer provides late-game fast travel, though discovering it late restructures a whole run's habits.

The cosy layer is the signature. Collectible grimlin companions carry personalities (healer, explorer, brave) and dig, heal and fight alongside the priestess; berries offer permanent stat boosts through real-time planting; fishing pads the quiet moments; hot springs with Sera deepen bonds; and Luna crystal offerings at shrines buy sigils. Sigils themselves are equippable passive and active abilities, and challenge rooms test builds. The whole apparatus wraps a demanding action core in a village heart that nothing else in the space quite matches.

Difficulty runs hotter than the serene presentation suggests. The bandit-occupied village of Mak is a genuine wall of punishing archers, the multi-invocation final fight against Selin drags through a long gauntlet of summon phases, and contact damage touches nearly everything. Enemies respawn on room transitions, the knockdown "vulnerable" status doubles incoming damage in a window that feels unfair, and the curse status blocks Bell healing at the worst moments. None of it breaks the run, but spikes are real.

Smaller friction accumulates: base movement is sluggish and sprint eats stamina, ruling it out as casual faster travel; the bow reads as a puzzle tool rather than a real second weapon; the endgame sigil loadout clutters the screen into a mess; Luna income feels thin against sigil prices; magic pickups demand a press-up rather than a walk-over; and the health bar repositions itself around the screen for no discernible reason. Each is minor. Together they are the honest cost of an otherwise polished package.

Overall, a gorgeous and genuinely demanding send-off to a long-running series, one whose pixel-art leap, ability-gated traversal curve and cosy companion-and-village layer reward a full completion that never demands its brutal postgame. Slow base movement, stamina-gated sprinting, contact damage and a fiery endgame are the acceptable costs of a package this warm, this pretty and this well tuned.
