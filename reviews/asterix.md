Astérix is a 1993 SNES platformer from Infogrames in which the Gaulish hero must rescue Obelix from the Romans. The campaign carries him through classic Astérix locales, from the village to Helvetia, Egypt, Greece and Rome.

The level structure is built around dozens of very short stages, many passable in around thirty seconds when the route is known. Generous time limits remove the usual retro pressure, and ten collected coins buy an extra life.

The music initially grates as a mind-numbing loop before becoming genuinely catchy, and the cave themes are a particular highlight. The absurd, rapid-fire deaths generate real amusement across a run that keeps throwing new surprises.

Hitboxes are the defining flaw, with the tiny attack range requiring direct contact to land a hit, which usually means taking damage in return. Enemies that cannot be harmed at all, such as the hedgehogs, compound the problem.

Forward visibility is poor, with the character sitting too far to one side of the screen. Ice physics and hazards that blend into the scenery make precision platforming unreliable, and hidden pathways can send the character back to the start of an area.

The pyramids act is the low point, described as worse than the caves and seemingly endless, with hidden traps waiting throughout. Save states feel necessary as frustration builds, yet the campaign still takes nearly two hours to complete.

Overall, hitbox design and the limited attack range undermine an otherwise charming, compact retro platformer. The catchy music and the short, numerous stages keep the campaign moving to its end.
