TR-49 is a narrative puzzle game from Inkle built around a book-eating machine of vacuum tubes and relays, hidden in the basement of a church. Built in 1943 to break German war codes, the machine ingests books and maps their titles to codes, with garbled pages clearing as each is assigned.

The deduction loop is the heart of the game, with codes built from authors' initials and years, and the player piecing together an archive of fictional journals and novels. Nothing about it is hard to understand; the challenge is tracking years, names and initials across many linked pages.

A red-dot indicator marks pages with undiscovered links, keeping the sprawling archive navigable. The maze is a choose-your-own-adventure style interlude of minotaurs, fountains and archways, and the only section that trips up reading.

The writing is dense and interconnected, with rivalries, thefts and family feuds among the fictional authors rewarding close attention. A distant soldier hunting through the building adds tension while the archive work continues.

The ending recontextualises the dialogue in a genuine reveal, and multiple endings plus a rewind-last-action feature encourage trying different choices. The controls are the main drawback, with finicky letter-and-number entry that is clearly better with a mouse.

It is easy to get sidetracked, since pages are crammed with links that lead to further links and the original thread is lost. The page list and the red dots help, and even unassigned pages can usually be read in full.

Overall, the code-breaking discovery loop is deeply satisfying and the archive lore is well-crafted. The controls and the sidetracking are the only real complaints.
