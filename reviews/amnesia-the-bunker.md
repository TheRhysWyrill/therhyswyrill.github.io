A World War I French bunker traps a soldier underground with a monstrous entity that hunts by sound, where darkness suffocates every corridor and the only illumination comes from a flashlight that generates noise. The premise establishes immediate survival pressure, with the generator powering overhead lights while fuel canisters scattered throughout the bunker demand constant scavenging. Environmental storytelling through notes and journals reconstructs the bunker's final hours, building atmosphere through fragments rather than exposition. From the opening descent, the claustrophobic architecture communicates danger at every turn.

Core gameplay revolves around the generator and flashlight mechanic, where light serves as both lifeline and liability. The Beast responds to sound, emerging from vents near noise sources and becoming significantly more active when the generator exhausts its fuel. Randomisation of codes, item placements, and trap locations ensures no two playthroughs follow identical patterns. Inventory management and crafting, including bandages and Molotov cocktails, add survival horror resource tension.

The generator mechanic stands as the most distinctive element, creating a constant push-pull between visibility and silence. When fuel runs dry, the bunker transforms into pitch darkness where the Beast patrols aggressively, forcing flashlight use that generates audible noise. The dynamic ensures every lighting decision carries consequence, sustaining tension across the full duration.

The Amnesia-style door control scheme, requiring held inputs and right-stick manipulation, creates friction during urgent navigation. Gas grenades appear functionally useless, offering no clear application against the Beast or environmental hazards. The campaign concludes within approximately six hours, appropriate for the experience but potentially insufficient for players seeking extended horror marathons.

Overall, Amnesia: The Bunker delivers a tightly designed survival horror experience where the generator-light mechanic transforms illumination into the primary source of danger, creating devious tension through noise-sensitive design.
