A wordless stealth-puzzle world of robotic corridors, chess-piece iconography, and a girl with time powers opens the story, told almost entirely without dialogue. Memento chapters with names like Caged, Silver Lining, Reunion, and Freedom sketch a tale of a test subject escaping experimentation, and the narrative trusts environmental storytelling throughout. The premise reads familiar on paper, but the mechanical treatment of time separates it from the Monument Valley and Prince of Persia comparisons it invites.

Time manipulation is the core verb, and the execution goes beyond a simple rewind. Pausing, scrubbing forward to preview enemy routes, rewinding to undo mistakes, and then committing to a plan makes every encounter a draft-and-revise process rather than a reaction test. The timeline scrubber presents the whole level's history on a horizontal slider, letting past actions be watched and re-edited like footage. Few puzzle games treat your own prior inputs as editable level content this thoroughly.

The cat companion elevates everything it touches. Introduced mid-game as a distract-the-robots tool and cat-flap key, it later becomes directly controllable, and its integration into both puzzles and story gives the quieter stretches an emotional anchor. Levels layer steadily around it: clickable footstep-hiding, cube-based bridge repair, distraction objects, and a disintegration power with limited charges all stack on the base verb set without ever overwhelming it.

The late-game echo mechanic is the standout flourish. Past attempts replay inside the level as ghosts, and puzzles are built around your own previous solutions, which differ for every player because the ghosts are your actions rather than scripted events. It turns the rewind system's history into puzzle content and lands as the campaign's most memorable idea, deepening rather than repeating what came before. Watching a discarded plan pay off in a later level gives the mechanic a payoff that scripted ghost systems never manage.

Character pathing is the most persistent friction. Set paths do not reset on rewind, so forgotten commands replay old mistakes, and the girl's deliberate slowness compounds the annoyance in a game where timing matters. The disintegrating-floor timed levels provide spectacle early but turn stressful and occasionally unfair-feeling late, and the relic challenge system never explains what its challenges actually are, leaving the collectibles opaque even after credits.

AI blind spots strain credibility while remaining exploitable. Robots fail to react to opening and closing doors and cannot see through glass, which enable solutions the design clearly intends but never justifies diegetically. The capture-the-robot-in-a-tiny-room puzzle demands almost no margin for error, forming a failure loop that tests patience more than reasoning, and the cat's shadow renders bugged in a brief visual wrinkle.

Length runs around three and a half hours for the five-chapter story, well under commonly cited estimates, and the campaign never outstays its ideas. Puzzle solutions stay open-ended throughout, with most levels admitting multiple valid approaches, and the game's creative identity holds from first puzzle to credits. Challenge relics pad completion for those inclined, though the mystery of what each one required goes unanswered by design or omission.

Overall, this is a short, relentlessly creative stealth-puzzler whose time-scrubbing hook, layered systems, and charming cat companion carry it well past its rough edges. Pathing that ignores rewinds, tight timed levels, and unexplained relics are the costs. A polished few hours where your own past plays become the puzzle, and the creativity never stops compounding.
