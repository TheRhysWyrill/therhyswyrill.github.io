World War I unfolds across five self-contained mini-campaigns, each following a different soldier through a distinct theatre of conflict. From the opening seconds, the tone announces itself through relentless violence: characters fall within moments of appearing, establishing that survival is never guaranteed. The anthology structure replaces a single narrative with mechanical variety, giving each story its own identity and gameplay focus.

Between the war stories, visual presentation remains consistently remarkable: destruction physics, particle effects, and environmental detail ground the chaos in a tangible world. The prologue's character-switching mechanic introduces the anthology format by cycling through soldiers in rapid succession.

The tank mission through muddy French trenches anchors the campaign with satisfying vehicular combat, where repair mechanics and varied encounters create a power fantasy that shifts perspective from infantry vulnerability to armoured dominance. The Italian armoured assault delivers a comparable rush, equipping a soldier in full plate armour with a devastating melee weapon.

Aviation sequences prove frustrating: tracking airborne enemies demands spatial awareness the minimap fails to communicate, and the train finale's artillery section compounds this by placing the player behind a gun that draws immediate fire. Checkpoint placement throughout later missions demands full retries from distant points.

Friendly fire identification remains problematic in chaotic engagements, with visual cues insufficient to distinguish allies from hostiles at distance. The pigeon messenger mechanic, by contrast, provides a surprisingly charming moment of levity amid the carnage.

Overall, the war story anthology delivers a military shooter with mechanical variety that persists across every campaign, even if aviation combat and checkpoint design create friction that interrupts the otherwise cinematic momentum.
