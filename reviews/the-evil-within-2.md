The Evil Within 2 is a survival horror sequel that swaps the corridor tension of its predecessor for a semi-open town packed with side content. The opening hours establish a more generous, action-friendly tone that the first game never attempted.

Stealth is the most improved mechanic, with crouched movement, corner takedowns and sound-aware enemies making quiet playthroughs genuinely viable. The original's clunky sneaking has been refined into a dependable tool that rewards patience and careful positioning.

Two large explorable areas and plenty of side quests give the game a welcome sense of freedom to wander. Crafting ammunition and healing items at workbenches, alongside separate upgrade trees for weapons and skills, keeps progression satisfying throughout.

The difficulty is significantly lower than the original, with standard enemies falling to a couple of headshots and resources rarely scarce. A full run can be completed without a single death, a stark contrast to the punishing opening of the first game.

Enemy design is a mixed bag, with memorable set-pieces such as the multi-headed creature and the stalking ghost lady outshining the generic boil-covered standard foes. Late-game flamethrower enemies soak up absurd amounts of ammunition and are easily the worst part of the combat.

The story gives Sebastian a personal stake, chasing his missing daughter through a collapsing simulated town while a camera-obsessed killer and a cult leader pull the strings. The writing can be cheesy, but the themes of guilt, grief and family keep the journey emotionally engaging.

Overall, the freedom to explore, the vastly improved stealth and the satisfying progression make this sequel far more enjoyable than its predecessor. It is barely a survival horror by the finish, but as an action-horror adventure it is well worth playing.
