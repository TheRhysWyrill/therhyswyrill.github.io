After Us sends a forest spirit across a dead Earth to free the life force trapped in eight animal vessels, with the released spirits roaming the world again once their hearts are cleansed. The wordless extinction story unfolds through the central arc, collectible spirit memories and the recurring revelation that the world's ruin came from human devourers.

Movement forms the backbone of the experience, with sprinting, double jumping, air dashes, wall riding and climbing combining into a kit praised throughout. A thrown heart that can be recalled interacts with elements, while the LifeLink reveals the locations of nearby spirits and collectables through song.

The eight areas each tie to an animal, with the hawk and eagle region by far the longest, and rescued spirits can be petted in the sanctuary hub. Fast travel between areas and dress-up options round out the hub, making completion hunting inviting.

Combat against hostile devourers appears sparingly, and shielded enemies force attacks from behind since the thrown heart breaks on contact. Most devourers with blue eyes can be cleansed instead of fought, keeping the emphasis on redemption over destruction.

The presentation is consistently smooth, with silky character animation, tree-growing sequences and a menagerie of animal designs standing out. Even in performance mode at 2K 60fps, moments of chugging appear, and the lack of a 1080p or 1440p frame-rate option drew complaint.

Occasional geometry snagging and balcony slides add friction to otherwise precise platforming, with a slightly higher jump sometimes needed. These irritations never derailed a run that reached every achievement and all eight vessels.

Overall, movement design makes After Us a smooth and distinctive action-adventure, with its extinction theme and animal sanctuaries lingering after the credits. The performance dips and platforming snags sit beneath a strong, memorable whole.
