Anomaly Agent delivers a stellar soundtrack and audio presentation, utilizing high-quality music during boss fights and character introductions alongside energetic guitar riffs that play whenever character portraits appear on screen. This vibrant presentation supports an exceptional art and character design, showcasing distinct themes for each chapter and incredibly cool introduction animations. The entire adventure hits all the right notes through its humor and writing, keeping the narrative engaging by constantly poking fun at itself through clever dialogue regarding character nicknames like the hooded man or white pants.

The core gameplay loop thrives on responsive mechanics, allowing you to fluidly dodge without resetting your active combos while utilizing a style-based execution mode alongside a variety of anomaly powers like the Mega Punch and Katana combination. Navigating these encounters is aided by strategic upgrades, letting you invest in poison-related perks that release toxic gas whenever you strike enemies or successfully parry incoming bullets. The level design remains consistently fresh through generous accessibility and checkpoints, resetting your health completely after every room to pair beautifully with creative mission objectives like a distinct stealth section involving a disguise.

However, this stylish brawler still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from technical input registration and crowded screen layouts. The gameplay suffers from frustrating technical input issues, where Steam input can occasionally register button presses twice to make precise movements difficult until the software feature is disabled. This structural friction is compounded by cluttered combat scenarios, making it exceptionally difficult to track the action or spot incoming attacks when too many enemies spawn at once or overlap on screen.

The user experience introduces minor headaches through specific enemy types, most notably shotgun-wielding foes whose attacks carry significant range and completely bypass your ability to parry them. You will also encounter inconsistent timing constraints, as the window for dodging can occasionally feel tight when warning indicators appear too late for you to properly react. This pacing friction is worsened by sluggish item management early in the campaign, resulting in a slow recharge time for essential items like the business card before you can unlock its proper speed upgrades.

Finally, the mechanical variety can occasionally feel dragged down by uninspired puzzle types, showcasing a lack of enthusiasm for certain logic-based puzzles that require tedious sequences just to lower blocks. Despite these minor structural complaints, the immediate focus remains squarely on immediate survival and high-energy combat. The generous progression loop ensures that the momentum never grinds to a complete halt even during the most demanding encounters.

Overall Anomaly Agent is a remarkably stylish, spectacular, and mechanically fluid action brawler that excels through its high-energy soundtrack, versatile anomaly powers, and witty self-aware writing. While the fast-paced combat is occasionally slowed down by chaotic enemy overlapping, unparriable shotgun attacks, sudden dodge timing spikes, and minor controller input glitches, the outstanding art style and rewarding upgrade system ensure it stands as a highly engaging and addictive beat em up experience.
