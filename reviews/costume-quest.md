Costume Quest drops siblings into a Halloween night trick-or-treating adventure when monsters kidnap one of them, opening with exploration across multiple neighbourhoods. Turn-based combat with timed button presses drives every encounter, with each costume offering different attacks and abilities that charge during battle. The Paper Mario influence runs deep, from timed hit mechanics to the badge-like battle stamp system that customises party capabilities.

Costume variety provides tactical depth, with each outfit delivering distinct offensive and defensive options against different enemy types. The battle stamps layer additional customisation, allowing experimentation with party builds across the campaign's escalating encounters. Exploration rewards careful trick-or-treating, with candy collection tied to progression through the neighbourhoods.

Dialogue auto-advance scrolls text too quickly to read comfortably, creating frustration during character interactions. The writing shines with witty observations and charming character moments, but the presentation undermines the delivery. Combat rarely feels repetitive due to the costume variety, though later stages introduce enemy compositions that demand loadout adjustments.

The final boss fight relies heavily on random elements, creating an anticlimactic conclusion to an otherwise polished experience. Boss encounters throughout the campaign maintain appropriate challenge, but the final confrontation's RNG dependency diminishes the sense of earned victory. Running at 60 FPS on PC delivers smooth visual presentation that enhances the turn-based combat's timed mechanics.

Multiple neighbourhoods provide environmental variety, each with distinct visual themes and encounter compositions. The world-building establishes a light-hearted Halloween atmosphere that sustains engagement across the campaign. Character interactions reinforce the sibling relationship through dialogue and shared objectives.

Overall, the Paper Mario-inspired combat and charming costume system deliver a turn-based RPG with lasting appeal, though dialogue auto-advance and the final boss's RNG dependency prevent the experience from reaching its full potential. The core mechanics remain satisfying throughout, anchored by the timed attack system and rewarding costume progression.
