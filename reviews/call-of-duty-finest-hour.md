Call of Duty: Finest Hour grounds its grim, urgent atmosphere in the multi-front perspectives of various soldiers fighting across disparate European battlefields. This narrative framing effectively provides weight to the setting, even when the underlying mission structure occasionally falters during the more demanding segments of the conflict.

Soviet and British chapters succeed in delivering consistently engaging scenarios that capture the raw intensity of historical combat with commendable focus. Players will likely find the initial hours buoyed by a well-realised sense of urgency that perfectly reflects the chaos of these harrowing military engagements.

Rigid escort objectives unfortunately dominate the later American campaign, creating a noticeable imbalance that stifles the momentum established during the opening acts. These tedious sequences force a shift from dynamic infantry skirmishes towards restrictive, repetitive tasks that struggle to maintain the excitement generated by previous, more fluid missions.

Visual clarity suffers significantly in darker, enclosed environments like the subterranean sewer levels due to low-resolution textures and a lack of brightness calibration. Navigating these murky tunnels becomes an unnecessary chore, as the absence of clear environmental cues forces players to stumble blindly through shadows during critical encounters.

Scarcity of health kits during the final act transforms challenging battles into an exhausting slog, highlighting a poorly tuned resource economy. Combat mechanics that felt functional earlier in the journey start to feel like liabilities when the game denies essential healing items during the most punishingly difficult showdowns.

Lumbering tank controls provide a brief shift in pace, yet the lethargic aiming speed turns precision targeting into a tiresome exercise. Modern quality-of-life staples like grenade indicators are noticeably missing, leaving combatants susceptible to sudden, unavoidable deaths that feel more like design oversights than intentional tests of skill.

Overall, this production remains an enjoyable diversion for fans of classic military shooters, best experienced if one accepts its inherent inconsistencies. While the strong atmosphere and early campaigns provide a compelling reason to enlist, the final hours test one’s patience with unforgiving spikes in difficulty and imbalanced mechanics.
