The trilogy's war for Earth opens with the homeworld burning, and Mass Effect 3 closes the saga with a scale no previous entry attempts. The fleet arrival and the Crucible reveal land as genuine spectacle, burning London hosts the final march, and harvesters and banshees fill the sky with grim atmosphere. The final assault and the march to the conduit carry real emotional weight, with the farewell tour of the ship's crew arriving strange but affecting. Every companion decision across three games pays off here, a structure no other entry in the series attempts. The ending's best outcomes respond to how the entire war was fought, making the finale genuinely earned rather than merely shown.

The Citadel DLC is the highlight of the entire game, dumb in the best way and worth nearly three hours of deliberate completion. Party sequences, arcade games and squad conversations stack into a huge send-off for the trilogy's entire cast, and no other entry gives its characters this kind of farewell. Squad size returns to a manageable few, a welcome correction from the previous game's twelve-strong bloat, and every character remains worth the smaller roster. The DLC alone justifies the three-pack re-release, alongside the writing that holds across all three games. As a capstone to a trilogy, the package is complete.

The extended cut resolves the infamous ending problem. Extra closure explains more of the finale's logic, and a sufficiently high war-asset score unlocks a survival stinger that transforms the whole ending's meaning. The Catalyst conversation presents three imperfect options, and the choice between them carries real cost, with the synthetic cost of the destructive path weighing genuinely. Reaching a high readiness score directly shapes the finale, giving the war's systems actual narrative consequence. The Leviathan reveal holds attention in the late stretches. The ending is only this satisfying with the extended cut and serious war-asset investment, a caveat the base release deserved not to need.

War assets and readiness systems reward engagement throughout, with the final score directly gating the best outcomes. Combat reaches power-fantasy territory by the endgame, with upgraded weapons tearing through brutes in a handful of shots, and loot micromanagement appropriately thins in the final stretch. Weapon mods and armour pieces expand build options, though the procurement weapon upgrades hide so effectively that an entire campaign can pass without touching them, a discovery better made in the first session than the last. The Citadel's arcade and party content adds depth beyond the war. Progression respects the trilogy's systems while simplifying its excesses.

The point-of-no-return before the final assault is badly signposted, locking players out of side content with no warning and punishing anyone saving the Citadel DLC for a post-finale treat. Discovering the lock after the fact stings, since the correct order was obvious in retrospect but never communicated. Neck-twisting talk animations unsettle repeatedly during the farewell tour, with NPCs rotating their heads unnaturally when approached from behind. Thinner endgame loot is arguably appropriate for the closing missions. These are small marks against an otherwise confident production.

Roughly forty-four hours covers a full completion, slightly shorter than the previous entry despite the DLC's heft, and the Citadel content accounts for much of the best of it. The farewell beats land because the trilogy earned them across three games of companion writing. Saying goodbye to everybody feels strange and right in equal measure. The combat's endgame power fantasy suits a story about overwhelming odds turning. As the closing chapter of an incredible trilogy, the game does everything a finale owes.

Overall, Mass Effect 3 is a fitting, thoroughly satisfying trilogy finale whose Citadel send-off, emotional farewell tour and responsive extended-cut ending deliver on three games of investment, held back only by badly signposted point-of-no-return gates and a handful of animation niggles. The ending rewards how the war was fought, and the trilogy as a whole stands as incredible games. A finale that earns its closing stinger.
