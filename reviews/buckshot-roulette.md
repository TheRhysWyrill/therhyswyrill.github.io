A waiver signed at the top of a staircase leads into a nightclub's underworld, where a serene, uncanny dealer waits across a table with a shotgun and a known shell ratio. There is no story beyond the descent, and none is attempted: three rounds against the dealer, buzzing fluorescents, pumping industrial music, and a finale staged in a spiky graveyard before the pearly gates. The grindhouse aesthetic commits completely, and the dealer's quiet menace carries every second of the encounter.

The odds loop is the entire game and it works. Shells load in a known ratio with unknown order, each turn allows item use before choosing to shoot the dealer or turn the gun on oneself, and blank self-shots grant an extra turn. Tracking live and blank counts aloud is the core verb, and the decision of who eats the next blast never loses its tension across the campaign's three rounds and the endless mode beyond.

Items layer probability management on top. Handcuffs skip the dealer's turn, the magnifying glass reveals the current shell, beer ejects one, the handsaw doubles damage, and cigarettes heal, with charges and defibs restoring health between rounds until the finale removes the safety net. Winning unlocks Double or Nothing, an endless mode that extends the loop indefinitely for anyone chasing the table for longer.

Item RNG is the swing factor that defines each run's length. A draw flooded with handcuffs cripples a run into tedium, while the ideal combination of cigarettes, beer, glass and saw trivialises the dealer entirely, and the distribution between those poles lands more on the frustrating side than the generous one. The campaign stretches or compresses almost entirely on which items arrive, not on skill growth.

The no-continue structure after death adds real stakes. Dying restarts the final round with no guaranteed healing items and the dealer back at full health, and an unlucky live-shell guess at close range can erase an entire run in one pull. The reset is harsh but legible, and the second-attempt win that follows delivers the payoff the loop builds toward.

Item behaviours go unexplained. Beer, magnifying glass and cigarettes are discovered by trial, the museum-adjacent mystery of what each object does is part of the fun, and the post-win statistics screen tracking beers and smokes used lands as throwaway filler. Neither issue weighs heavily against the loop, but a brief tutorial hint would cost nothing.

Overall, the distilled odds-reading loop, grimy presentation and pure tension make this a remarkably tight package, and the item RNG swings and brevity are honest costs rather than betrayals of the concept. Nothing else distils probability horror this cleanly, and the endless mode stretches the table as far as anyone wants to take it.
