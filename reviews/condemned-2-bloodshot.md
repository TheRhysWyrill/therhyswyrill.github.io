A former serial crimes investigator, now homeless and reliant on alcohol, walks back into a city sliding into open riot. Sonic emitters pulse beneath the chaos. Melee brawling and forensic deduction compete for attention from the outset.

Jabs, hooks, parries and chain attacks flow into environmental finishes, with weakened enemies shoved headfirst through televisions. Improvised weapons splinter after extended use, forcing constant scavenging. Firearms hold only what fits in the magazine and nothing more. Blocking and counter-timing matter as much as aggression.

Alcohol dependence doubles as an aiming mechanic, since steady hands require a drink before any trigger pull. Forensic sections break the brawling with ultraviolet sweeps and blood spatter interpretation. Optional objectives earn bronze, silver or gold ratings that set the size of each upgrade. Antenna tuning drip-feeds extra lore.

Goo fiends introduce the sharpest friction, feinting attacks before withdrawing, then landing frequent hits despite modest damage. Visibility compounds the problem, with a feeble flashlight leaving rooms almost unreadable. Head bobbing during sprints adds its own discomfort. A lone dog encounter lands as an unwelcome interruption.

News broadcasts from reporter Tony Rhodess punctuate the descent, charting a city abandoned by police. Scarce light and constant audio threats keep corridors tense. The emitter mystery provides momentum between fights through radio conversations that hint without confirming. Even remembered sections fray nerves.

Historical instability hangs over the PC version, with crashes and soft locks dogging earlier attempts, yet this run holds together. Floating items occasionally mar the visuals. Tutorials crowd the first levels before brawling takes over. Weapons snapping mid-fight keep pressure high.

Overall, deep melee combat, meaningful upgrades and a relentlessly dark city make this a sequel that improves on its foundations. Every system, from brawling to forensics, pulls in the same direction.
