Clawville teeters on collapse three years after the fall of the Whistler Empire, and a rooster detective duo is hired by a mysterious insect woman from the segregated Hive to find her husband's stolen corpse. The case spirals outward into a conspiracy involving a cult led by Lady Kayla, the Golden Fang clan, a suspicious meat-substitute plant and a crown spy hiding in plain sight, and the escalation from missing-corpse procedural to political intrigue is handled with le Carré-worthy discipline. Hand-painted portraits, full voice acting and a hardboiled pastiche register complete the package. Few games commit this thoroughly to animal-noir, and the commitment pays off.

Writing and character dialogue are the engine of the entire experience. Exchanges between the two detective partners and their collaborators reward slowing down, and the banter lands with a sharpness most narrative adventures never approach. Interrogations are built on good-cop/bad-cop dialogue choices where asking the best questions at the best time opens or breaks suspects, and replaying options tactically is genuinely engaging rather than busywork. Interview choices unlock or lock questions, giving conversations real stakes.

Noir presentation carries the tone, supported by voice acting that is uniformly top-tier across the roster. The sequel adds an optional colour mode alongside the stark monochrome noir presentation, and the choice is almost unheard of in the medium: vibrancy versus shadow, decided by the viewer. Colour adds life to Clawville, but the monochrome mode remains the stronger identity, and the game wisely permits both. There is a genuine argument the original's stark aesthetic suited the material better, yet having the option is no bad thing.

The codex auto-fills with lore as locations and suspects are examined, and the density is double-edged. Religious insect cults, the Hive's segregation, post-empire politics and faction histories pile up fast, and the information flow can overwhelm on first contact. Secrets scattered through scenes reward thoroughness, and a shooting-range minigame offers an occasional change of pace, though its aiming is sluggish and imprecise. The codex rewards rereading more than the moment-to-moment exploration suggests.

Pacing is the campaign's structural weakness. The prologue and opening chapters run long and meaty, the middle pair sustain that density, and then the final two chapters collapse into half an hour each, abrupt by comparison with everything that came before. The whiplash is real: hours of methodical interrogation followed by a sprint through the climax. What should build to a payoff instead rushes past the material that deserved the game's fullest attention.

The handling of a returning character's death is the sequel's strangest misstep. Bringing back a figure from the earlier case only to stage his demise with minimal buildup and an immediate funeral lands as bizarre rather than impactful, and the decision reads as pointless in retrospect. By contrast, the mid-game spy reveal, in which an apparently loyal character is exposed as a crown informant, genuinely surprises and elevates the conspiracy. The two beats sit uneasily together: one twist earns its shock, the other squanders its setup.

Friction aside, the whole remains greater than the flawed ending. The investigation loop, the dialogue craft and the sheer commitment to the noir register carry the weaker final hours, and the epilogue's quieter beats land well after the abruptness. As a sequel it honours its predecessor while expanding the world in scale and colour, and the one-and-done linear structure suits a mystery of this shape. The finale stumbles; the journey justifies it.

Overall, this is a stylish, beautifully written animal-noir mystery whose razor-sharp dialogue, superb voice acting and surprising spy plotting far outweigh whiplash pacing and a bafflingly staged death at the finish line. The final chapters rush what deserved patience, but everything preceding them makes the case that few studios commit this hard to a register and land it this often.
