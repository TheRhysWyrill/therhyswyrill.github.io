A sci-fi city crumbles under the influence of resar, a substance functioning as both technology and narcotic. Detective Jesse Sullivan investigates anomalies caused by this energy, leading into urban decay and otherworldly environments. Twin-stick shooting drives the core loop, with three distinct characters offering different approaches to combat encounters.

Each character brings a unique toolkit: the detective wields a shocker, dash, and energy barrier; Basel commands teleportation and grenades; Helga relies on a bow, drone, and grappling hook. Switching between them mid-combat allows adaptation to different enemy configurations, and the generous health system ensures experimentation remains viable.

Combat starts confusing but rewards persistence, as the control scheme feels unconventional at first with movement and aiming requiring adjustment. Once the mechanics click, encounters become satisfying, particularly during large enemy waves where the resar beam decimates groups with devastating efficiency.

The resar beam weapon stands as the most powerful tool in the arsenal. It tears through enemies with visual flair, though using it restricts movement entirely. This trade-off creates tactical decisions about when to unleash its power versus maintaining mobility during chaotic encounters.

Platforming sections appear in pyramid levels, demanding precise jumps and timing. These sequences rely on trial-and-error learning, where failure teaches the correct approach through repetition. Enemy behaviour compounds this challenge, as hostile units can redirect mid-charge with minimal warning.

Music enhances the action throughout, with energetic tracks that complement the fast-paced shooting. Environmental design shifts between neon-lit urban streets and hellish underworld arenas, each with distinct visual identities that reinforce the escalating threat.

Overall, Re.Surs delivers a twin-stick shooter with satisfying character switching and a memorable resar beam weapon, though the initial learning curve and trial-and-error platforming segments create friction that smooths out over time.
