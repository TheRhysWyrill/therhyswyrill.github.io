Conarium establishes an eerie cyber-archaeological atmosphere across an isolated Antarctic research base where ancient pre-human relics trigger deeply unsettling cosmic visions. Waking up with severe memory loss after undergoing experiments with a mysterious device forces the protagonist to piece together a missing expedition through scattered journals and ancient scripts.

Progression relies on utilising specialised tools like flashlights, mechanical cranks, and glowing crystal artefacts to power ancient machinery and clear blocked pathways. Solving these environmental logic puzzles requires carefully analysing world-based clues and historical documentation to make sense of the forgotten subterranean architecture buried far beneath the ice.

Exploration extends into icy subterranean waters during dedicated vehicle segments where players steer a small submarine through dark underwater tunnels using light beacons. Navigating these claustrophobic aquatic pathways offers a refreshing change of pace while unearthing deeper, more terrifying layers of the ancient alien complex hidden below the facility.

Unorthodox default button mappings and camera friction introduce noticeable mechanical clumsiness while manipulating intricate spatial puzzles or inspecting environmental objects up close. Navigating tight physical spaces occasionally feels awkward, yet solid technical performance guarantees that transitions between base corridors and underwater caverns remain consistently smooth.

Atmospheric dread relies on a quiet, slow-burn mood heavily inspired by classical cosmic horror literature rather than frantic physical combat encounters. Sudden jump scares occasionally disrupt this subtle environmental tension, feeling slightly uninspired compared to the genuine psychological fear generated by reading detailed research logs.

Storytelling relies heavily on external lore logs and detailed research notes, leaving the silent protagonist to absorb tragic discoveries strictly through quiet observation. Collecting optional trophies and hidden secret items adds pleasant narrative flavour for inquisitive players without creating any unnecessary roadblocks along the primary investigative path.

Overall, environmental logic puzzles and rich research documents synthesize to create a deeply absorbing cosmic horror investigation across the frozen Antarctic ruins. Overcoming clunky interaction controls and occasional jump-scare interruptions reveals a thoroughly rewarding expedition that consistently satisfies dedicated fans of atmospheric narrative exploration.
