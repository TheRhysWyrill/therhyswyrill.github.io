An anthropologist awakens in a deserted Antarctic research base after a mysterious experiment, piecing together what happened through notes, journals, and visions. The atmosphere is thick with Lovecraftian dread through dark corridors, alien ruins, and cosmic horror themes that create genuine tension throughout the exploration.

The Canarium device serves as the central mechanic for accessing visions and alternate dimensions, with the concept of transcending human consciousness providing genuinely intriguing narrative depth. The device's dual function of seeing and being seen creates philosophical questions that extend beyond the immediate survival horror.

Environmental puzzles require matching symbols, operating machinery, and navigating darkened environments through flashlight illumination. The torch mechanic adds atmospheric tension by forcing the player to choose between visibility and horror, with the darkness beyond the beam creating genuine unease during exploration sequences.

Puzzle controls prove frustrating and unintuitive, particularly during the desert ring puzzle where the interaction mechanics feel clunky and imprecise. The controls undermine the tension that the atmosphere builds, creating frustration during sequences that should maintain immersion rather than break it.

The submarine navigation section proves difficult to control and visually unclear, with ambiguous directional cues making underwater progression uncertain. The section demonstrates the game's willingness to experiment with different environments, though the execution falls short of the atmospheric standards established elsewhere.

The rich lore and world-building through collectible notes and documents draw deeply from Lovecraft mythology, creating genuine investment in the game's universe. The documents provide context that transforms exploration from simple navigation into discovery, with each note revealing new layers of the cosmic horror narrative.

Overall, the atmospheric tension and Lovecraftian lore establish a compelling horror adventure, though frustrating puzzle controls and the submarine section prevent the experience from fully realising its potential as an immersive cosmic horror experience.
