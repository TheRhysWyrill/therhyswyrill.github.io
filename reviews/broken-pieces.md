Elise finds herself alone in the abandoned French coastal village of St. Exel, piecing together what happened to the townspeople through scattered journals, tape recordings, and environmental clues. The premise establishes a cult mystery involving a mysterious organisation called the Devotees, setting the stage for methodical exploration against an ever-present sense of dread.

Environmental storytelling drives the experience through personal belongings scattered across weathered streets, handwritten notes from frightened residents, and cassette tape recordings that layer Elise's thoughts over the investigation. The seasonal switching mechanic, triggered via fountains and Rocky the magic stone, reveals entirely different environmental states (frozen waterways, collapsed walls, hidden paths) that create satisfying puzzle chains.

The seasonal switching mechanic rewards observation, with summer revealing accessible structures that winter collapses into new traversal puzzles. Solving multi-step environmental chains involving axes, circuit breakers, and levers opens progression through the village's interconnected districts. Combat punctuates these sequences but lacks the precision of the deduction systems, with a twirl-based dodge that only works directionally and feels unreliable against certain enemy attacks.

The shotgun arrives late and provides instant kills but feels like an afterthought rather than a meaningful upgrade. Bomb mechanics appear underutilised and poorly explained, adding confusion rather than depth to the encounters. Some collectibles like lost coins and pattern game items feel like filler compared to the environmental storytelling.

Overall, the seasonal weather-switching mechanic and environmental puzzle chains create a genuinely distinctive exploration experience, though clunky combat and occasional technical instability prevent the atmosphere from fully establishing itself.
