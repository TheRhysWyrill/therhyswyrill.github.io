Extinction places players in the role of a lone warrior tasked with defending crumbling cities from a relentless horde of gargantuan threats. This premise initially promises a grand spectacle of desperate heroism, yet the experience quickly unravels as the mechanical limitations and design flaws become impossible to ignore.

Fluid movement defines the traversal, allowing Avil to sprint across rooftops with impressive speed and agility. Unfortunately, these moments of mobility are shackled to a mission structure that rarely deviates from a strictly formulaic pattern of rescuing civilians and hacking away at the same predictable ogre-like Ravenni foes.

Rune energy serves as a critical resource, yet it is balanced so poorly that progression feels actively punitive rather than challenging. Executing a successful strike drains this energy entirely, leaving defenders unable to counter further threats when the drops from standard enemies or civilians fail to materialise at necessary intervals.

Xandra provides constant, intrusive audio prompts that repeat warnings about the city’s health even during the most frantic moments of engagement. This incessant feedback loop adds unnecessary irritation to an already stressful situation, especially when the extinction meter feels destined to collapse regardless of how efficiently the player behaves.

Combat lacks the surgical precision required for a game focused on battling massive, agile opponents. While the rune strike system appears tactical upon first encounter, it ultimately feels clunky and shallow when compared to more refined titles that successfully capture the thrill of fighting skyscraper-sized enemies with cinematic flair.

Awkward tutorials appear sporadically throughout the campaign, often explaining vital mechanics only after the player has already blundered through relevant encounters. Such poor user interface design compounds the feeling of confusion, leaving one to navigate an increasingly impossible difficulty curve without the necessary tools or clear guidance to succeed.

Overall, redundant objectives and flawed balance ensure that this desperate defence of humanity remains a largely unrewarding exercise. Players seeking an engaging experience will find little depth here to sustain interest, as the repetitive loops and unfair resource gating render the journey into a tedious cycle of frustration and failure.
