A post-cataclysm sci-fi city swallowed by reality-warping domes sets the stage for a compact search for a missing sister, and the artefact at the centre of the search turns out to be the game's entire mechanical identity. The city of Modigaard unfolds through environmental vignettes, with newspapers, telescopes, microphones and framed photographs each tied to a memory of the absent sister, lending the short campaign surprising emotional texture. A sarcastic AI companion inhabiting the transmitter lightens the tone with dry banter, while mysterious crow shades drift through the margins adding mythological intrigue. The Mega Man Zero lineage announces itself through boss warnings and stage flow. What could be a shallow homage gains depth from how completely the conceit integrates with the story.

Shield throwing is the heart of the toolkit, hurling the energy bracelet at switches and enemies with a heft that never wears thin. Wall rebounding redirects the shield off surfaces into awkward angles, shield-surfing turns downhill slopes into transit, and bouncing off surfaces extends traversal in ways the level design rewards. Blocking projectiles charges a special attack, folding defence into offence with a satisfying rhythm. A simple three-hit combo followed by a throw clears most encounters with ease, a generosity that suits the game's breezy disposition. Every ability stems from the single defensive tool, and the fusion is the design's central strength.

Parry and dodge logic comes to the fore during the hospital bot fight, a mid-game encounter that refreshes the combat loop once its rhythm clicks. Bosses gate each area, swinging between breezy and demanding: the immortal self-repairing autonomous mecha is a highlight backed by superb music, and later fights resolve quickly once their patterns are understood. The finale layers an escape chase sequence and a dual-boss fight onto the standard formula, escalating well for a campaign this compact. Bottomless pits drain health rather than killing outright, a merciful choice that keeps momentum flowing. Generous checkpoints support the forgiving disposition throughout.

The first crow boss is the campaign's low point, with a win condition signposted so poorly that several deaths pass before the block-into-special rhythm reveals itself. Once the logic lands the fight softens, but the initial opacity sits awkwardly against the otherwise fair encounter design. Opaque boss telegraphs recur occasionally, asking for experimentation the rest of the game rarely demands. The soundtrack deserves particular praise, earning admiration at nearly every boss encounter with tracks that punch well above the game's size. Few short platformers invest this heavily in their battle themes.

The post-game collectible hunt sends memory items across revisitable levels through the level select, a pleasant reason to return until the final items arrive with almost no guidance about which area hides them. Cross-referencing outside resources becomes the only practical route to completion, an avoidable frustration in an otherwise tidy package. The game's listing of partial controller support is inaccurate in the opposite direction, with every button mapped and functioning, though the absence of d-pad movement remains a genuinely odd quirk. A 100% run completes in roughly two and a half hours, a length that fits the scope precisely.

The bracelet's true significance lands in the final act, reframing every block, throw and surf that came before as something far more personal than a weapon slot. Revealing the nature of the connection would rob a payoff that works precisely because the game never telegraphs it, and the moment earns the emotional weight the memory vignettes have been quietly accumulating. Esther's search resolves with genuine warmth. It is a small twist, executed cleanly, and it elevates the whole campaign around it.

Overall, this is a short, well-tuned retro throwback whose shield toolkit, excellent boss music and forgiving design earn their two and a half hours comfortably. The bracelet conceit fuses offence, traversal and story resolution into a single object with unusual completeness. The opaque first boss and under-signposted collectibles are real blemishes, yet the whole remains a simple, enjoyable package that knows exactly how big it wants to be.
