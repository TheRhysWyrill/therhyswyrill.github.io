A near-future Hong Kong cyberpunk police procedural wrapped around a Ninja Gaiden-style brawler should not work, and Wanted: Dead makes no effort to reconcile the halves. Hannah Stone leads the misfit Zombie Squad, a squad of convicted war criminals rehabilitated into police service, investigating synthetics and corporate conspiracies across Dollar Synthetics, Continental Standard and Engel security. The tone swings wildly between a Hong Kong action film, sudden idol-style anime cutscenes and a gore system whose persistence carries into cutscenes, and the whiplash reads as deliberate rather than accidental. Little of the story decodes cleanly, and the anime interludes stay confusing throughout, but the confidence of the nonsense carries its own charm.

Katana melee is the fun core. Dash attacks launch enemies flying, parries open finishers, limb-severing executions scatter the battlefield, and zipping around slicing and dicing never stops being the reason to keep going. The gore earns its reputation: limbs fly off and armless enemies keep fighting, a grim absurdity the game commits to without blinking. Skill points unlock bigger parry windows, extra finishers and grenade capacity, and two extra combos round out the katana's kit, though the unlock ceiling arrives quickly.

Gunplay is the weaker half. Shooting feels awkward next to the melee, the skill ceiling sits shallow, and cover behaves inconsistently under pressure. Weapon customisation compounds the confusion with stats that defy logic, leather grips that reduce damage and magazine sizes that alter accuracy, choices that make no sense however they are read. Gun-counters and the SMG, shotgun and LMG options fill out the arsenal, but the sword remains the answer to almost every problem.

The HQ interludes define the game's unhinged personality. Ramen-eating rhythm games, karaoke, crane machines, a shooting-range score attack and a retro side-scrolling shooter cabinet interrupt the violence at regular intervals, and each one lands as genuine variety rather than filler. The seamless nightclub transition into anime is the standout presentation moment, even as the recurring waking-in-anime-land framing device stays inexplicable. A Big Smoke-style breakfast order opens the campaign, a tutorial avalanche follows, and the identity confusion around the Zombie Squad name, which promises zombies that never arrive, niggles from the first hour.

Difficulty sours the back half. Grenade and grenade-launcher one-shots come relentlessly, insta-kill boss attacks punish without warning, and checkpoint placement leaves long stretches to replay, a combination that turns the ending stretch frustrating even while the combat itself stays fun. Shield soldiers, minigun heavies and grenade-launcher ambushes stack the late levels, and the final boss's unparryable triple-hit combos demand repeated retries. The nightclub level doubles as the campaign's technical low point, where the frame rate dies amid destructible bottles because the game cannot handle its own prettiness.

The finale lands with an ambiguous standoff and a post-credit flashback hinting at the protagonist's past, a Japanese Hard difficulty tease closing the credits as a punchline. The story's scattered collectible notes and squad banter fill in the corporate conspiracy around the mysterious John Doe, and the whole narrative stays enjoyably incoherent rather than dull. For all its jank, nothing here is boring.

Overall, Wanted: Dead is a gory, tonally unhinged character-action curiosity whose katana melee, minigame digressions and sheer commitment to strangeness make it easy to enjoy despite awkward gunplay, nonsensical weapon stats and a brutal final act. It is janky, it is strange, and it is fun, which turns out to be enough.
