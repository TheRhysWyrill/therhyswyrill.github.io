A young woman named Ash enters the spirit world after a spirit steals her mother's pendant, establishing immediate narrative stakes through loss and supernatural mystery. The premise unfolds through exploration and dialogue with a cast of spirit characters, each carrying distinct personalities and stories. From the opening interactions, the pixel-art aesthetic creates a warm visual identity that balances lighthearted character moments with darker themes. The atmosphere shifts between cheerful exchanges and moments involving curses and sacrifice.

Core gameplay involves exploring environments, talking to characters, and solving simple puzzles through item collection and environmental interaction. The progression relies on conversation and observation rather than mechanical complexity, with narrative advancement tied to relationship-building with spirit inhabitants. The game remains relatively short but packs significant narrative depth through its character interactions and hidden connections. Door and stair interactions occasionally feel unintuitive but do not significantly disrupt the exploration flow.

The shared universe connections stand as the most distinctive element, linking Ashina to the developer's previous works through narrative revelations that reward attentive players. The charming cast of spirit characters creates an engaging world where each conversation adds depth to the setting. The narrative balances humour with genuine emotional moments, creating atmosphere that sustains engagement despite mechanical simplicity.

Dialogue audio introduces a loud, jarring noise when text loads, creating repetitive disruption during character interactions. The gameplay remains primarily walking and talking, which may not satisfy players seeking mechanical complexity or extended gameplay duration. The simple puzzle design, while accessible, lacks challenge that might deepen engagement beyond the narrative.

Overall, Ashina: The Red Witch delivers a charming narrative experience where surprising universe connections and character-driven storytelling create genuine emotional depth, despite mechanical simplicity that limits appeal beyond story-focused players.
