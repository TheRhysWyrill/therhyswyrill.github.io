A festival favour frames a free film-car collection hunt across a compact coastal map. Handler Tez tasks a driver with earning a ten-car shopping list for film-star Ludacris by proving skill in Nice, Saint-Martin and the Aerodrome. Pink-slip duels against car owners and bucket-list skill challenges gate the roster before a convoy cargo-plane finale. Tone stays light promo fantasy built on car culture, heists and film-car worship, with handler radio chatter supplying the only narrative texture. Stripped progression and absent customisation keep systems thin, so the cars and showcases carry the weight.

Eight named film machines anchor the garage, from muscle iron to hypercar excess. The Charger, Supra, Road Runner and Cuda deliver old-school muscle attitude with tail-happy handling. A GTR, McLaren P1 and Veyron answer with precision and straight-line savagery, while the Maserati Ghibli splits the difference. Mid-hunt preferences tilt toward the precision camp, since muscle cars punish mistakes on narrow checkpoint layouts. Dynamic weather adds texture the older console build lacked, with rain slicking surfaces and changing grip. Straight-line speed and cornering grip stay approachable throughout, so ambition earns more than caution.

Drive-to-event racing forms the core loop, with each trip to a start line doubling as map exploration. One-shot nitrous equalises duels through a single event-only burst that forbids free-roam use and never regenerates. Rewind assists forgive single mistakes without removing the need for clean lines. Skill chains reward sustained sideways momentum, while speed traps, speed zones and delivery runs diversify the hunt. Barn-find recovery hides machinery off the beaten path for thorough explorers.

Showcase events stage the most outlandish set-pieces of the hunt, headlined by a Jeep-versus-helicopter duel. Street races through Nice thread traffic and tight corners into pack battles. Saint-Martin roads open into faster countryside blasts where the hypercars stretch their legs. The Aerodrome hosts flat-out speed tests that reward commitment over caution. Bucket-list targets layer bespoke skill goals over familiar tarmac to extend each location.

Rainy handling defines the driving challenge, with wet surfaces punishing abrupt inputs. Off-road bumps unsettle cars that stray from tarmac, demanding line discipline on mixed-surface routes. Narrow checkpoint placement punishes wide entries more than rival pace ever does. Drivatar opposition rarely threatens, trailing the real challenges of weather and checkpoint discipline. Conditions and checkpoint placement thus form the true opposition, leaving the starting grid as backdrop.

PC performance impresses with smooth, native-feeling running that outclasses the older console build and the troubled UWP Horizon 3 experience. Preservation matters here, since this delisted standalone promo now persists in the strongest form of the 360-era original. Road discovery falters on faint gray-on-white fog-of-war that hides the last undriven sliver and turns late cleanup into a tedious search. Constant GPS rerouting chatter nags exploration, repeating safe-turn prompts at every collected board and reward. Missing customisation and economy options thin long-term tinkering, though the hunt never pretends to offer them. Bonus boards and road mileage reward wandering between events, even while the GPS objects.

Festival atmosphere leans on engine notes, paint and pageantry rather than story. Handler radio chatter keeps the list moving without deepening character. Film-car worship supplies the emotional hook, each earned machine landing as a trophy. Light heist framing dresses straightforward races in cinematic clothing. The closing convoy sequence escalates scale without resolving anything beyond the list itself. Delisted status lends the package a time-capsule quality, preserving a promo that can no longer be bought.

Overall, the ten-car hunt charms through film machines, helicopter spectacle and one-shot NOS drama, with rain-slicked roads and barn-find detours sustaining momentum. Faint discovery fog and GPS nagging blur late cleanup, but the free promo delivers its cars with handling that flatters every event.
