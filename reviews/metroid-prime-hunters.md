A telepathic message from a dying civilisation sends six rival bounty hunters racing for an ultimate power, and Metroid Prime Hunters condenses that Prime-era premise onto dual screens with more success than the hardware has any right to allow. The Alimbic lore, the imprisoning seal sphere and the hunter-vs-hunter framing slot the game between the first two Prime entries, reusing species from both in a way that reads strange but charming. As a handheld miniature of the Prime formula it is genuinely solid, a surprise given the platform. Eight Octoliths, each behind a boss and a timed escape, structure the campaign, wrapped in a surprisingly open planet-hopping layout with fast travel via portals.

The single most important adjustment is the control scheme itself. Swapping stylus aiming for face buttons transforms the game from an exercise in cramping one finger into something genuinely playable, and the swap is the difference between enduring and enjoying the campaign. Without it, the clunky mess underneath shows: no lock-on aiming of any kind, erratic fast-moving bosses, and combat that fights the interface as often as the enemies. So much of the friction would dissolve with a simple lock-on, and its absence is the game's most consequential design gap. With the remap in place, though, the core loop holds up remarkably well.

Scanning and lore logging are satisfying and generous, with scan logs delivering Prime-style worldbuilding that ranges from zombie ecology jokes to unexpectedly heavy phrasing. Energy drop rates run higher than series norms, with pink pickups granting thirty and blue ones sixty, keeping exploration forgiving. Weapons are unusually plentiful, possibly the most of any entry: six beams, six special weapons, the power beam and missiles, each gated behind coloured forcefield barriers that give the exploration a clear key-and-lock rhythm. Notably, there are no suit upgrades at all, likely a series first, which trims the progression tree without hollowing it.

Vesper Defence Outpost introduces ice physics without warning, turning its floors into a skating rink of momentum, and the frosty Arcterra proxy-lock hunt extends the variety further. The campaign's most hated stretch is a mandatory timed bomb-jump morpball puzzle, where the bombs take an age to detonate and the timing window collapses under the delay. Fall damage, rare for the series, and instant-death piston rooms add cheap-death friction, tempting shortcuts around the rules. The checkpoint system, by contrast, is more generous than expected and rescues several runs that the piston rooms threaten.

Boss quality is a genuine mixed bag. The early Slench tower encounter is fine, but the erratic, fast-moving later variants, Slench v2 and v3 and Cretaphid v4, become exercises in fighting the controls rather than the bosses themselves. Slench v2 is the low point, nearly impossible thanks to a tiny hitbox, erratic movement patterns and the missing lock-on. The six rival hunters who ambush mid-exploration produce one standout disaster, a timed three-minute escape against Sylux that sours a stretch of otherwise enjoyable play. No health drops during boss fights compound the difficulty spikes unfairly.

The multiplayer-flavoured DNA shows in the structure, with hunters ambushing during exploration giving the solo campaign an adversarial edge no other entry replicates. The ambushing keeps routes tense even when the fights themselves are uneven. Between the Octolith hunts, the portal network and the weapon-gated doors, the pacing alternates exploration and confrontation in a rhythm closer to the mainline than the spin-off label suggests. The scan-lore generosity ties it together, rewarding every corner with a log entry.

Overall, Metroid Prime Hunters is a genuinely solid portable Prime compromised by DS-era controls that turn its worst bosses into fights against the game itself, with the face-button remap rescuing an experience generous in weapons, scan lore and exploration. Slench v2, Cretaphid v4 and one timed ambush leave a sour taste, but the core holds. Judge it as a condensed Prime and it earns its place.
