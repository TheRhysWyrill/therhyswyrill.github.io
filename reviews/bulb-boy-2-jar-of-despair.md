Bulb Boy 2: Jar of Despair delivers expanded technical features, introducing full controller support as a welcome improvement over the original title to provide more flexibility in how the game is played. This bizarre adventure benefits from an enhanced art direction, shifting from a mostly monochrome green aesthetic to a vibrant, full-color world that makes the detailed environments look significantly more impressive. Discovering solutions remains highly engaging due to creative puzzle logic, showcasing clever points of interaction like using a sticky cobweb as a lasso or filling a dirty sock with water to grow a giant plant.

The core loop feels consistently fresh by embracing varied gameplay styles, seamlessly switching between traditional puzzle-solving, first-person shooting galleries, and controlling different companion characters like a loyal dog or a bug-killing tablet. These sequences showcase compelling character designs that remain visually interesting despite their grotesque nature, ranging from an egg-headed mother to bizarre sausage-fingered transformations. Exploration is further aided by improved navigation logic, including helpful pathfinding cues where your dog can mark a trail to lead you through a complex maze, all wrapping up in a fair game length of around ninety minutes.

However, this surreal horror comedy still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from its sluggish interface and obtuse design choices. The gameplay suffers from a frustratingly slow cursor speed during high-pressure segments, creating a major bottleneck in the shooting gallery where the unadjustable reticle tracking speed frequently leads to unnecessary character deaths. This friction is compounded by obscure puzzle logic, featuring overly obtuse sequences that require a highly specific character to eat a sandwich just to remove tiny flea irritants from another party member.

The user experience introduces minor headaches through inconsistent interaction cues, as critical progression items like a screwdriver can blend too perfectly into background clutter. You will also encounter unclear failed state feedback during intense boss fights, leaving you completely confused as to why an attack failed or how to avoid a sudden one-hit kill from an aggressive monster. This issue leads to a repetitive trial-and-error loop during timed puzzles, forcing you into tedious perfect execution runs across multiple failed attempts to align a pizza slice or complete a twin-character boss fight.

Combat can become exceptionally punishing due to a total lack of an enemy attack cooldown, resulting in an annoying stun-locking system where your character is hit multiple times in rapid succession without any chance to escape. Finally, the visual presentation makes some highly questionable stylistic decisions regarding the character models. The choice to give absolutely every character an emphasized and bizarrely pronounced butt crack is incredibly distracting, ultimately serving as an odd visual gag that fails to add anything meaningful to either the horror or the comedy.

Overall Bulb Boy 2: Jar of Despair is a remarkably vibrant, mechanically varied, and creatively designed puzzle adventure that excels through its gorgeous full-color upgrade, surreal character concepts, and clever point-and-click logic. While the grotesque journey is occasionally bogged down by sluggish cursor speeds, obtuse item combinations, frustrating enemy stun-locks, and bizarre character art details, the concise pacing and entertaining gameplay variety ensure it stands as a highly enjoyable and memorable sequel for fans of quirky indie horror.
