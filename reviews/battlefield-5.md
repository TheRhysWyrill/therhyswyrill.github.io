Battlefield V replaces a single WWII narrative with five standalone war stories, each following different soldiers across distinct theatres of conflict. North African stealth operations, Norwegian resistance, French liberation, and German tank warfare all receive individual treatment, creating an anthology that refuses to privilege one perspective. Native language dialogue across these stories adds authenticity that distinguishes the campaign from typical military shooters.

Between missions, visual presentation holds up well across diverse environments, with destruction physics remaining consistent and the variety of settings preventing visual fatigue.

The Last Tiger war story provides the campaign's strongest experience: tank gameplay combines satisfying vehicular combat with a perspective that challenges typical WWII narratives. Driving a Mark V through crumbling streets, engaging enemy armour while managing ammunition and positioning, creates momentum that persists across the mission's structure. The German viewpoint adds nuance without requiring narrative sympathy.

Stealth mechanics in Under No Flag function reasonably well, with silenced weapons and detection systems providing viable alternatives to frontal assault. Technical issues, however, interrupt the flow: game crashes, locked loadouts, and progression blockers force restarts that reset progress to earlier points.

The menu system represents the campaign's most persistent frustration, with cluttered interfaces and unintuitive navigation that slow basic actions. Binoculars operate at a crawl, and white objective markers against light backgrounds render navigation nearly impossible in outdoor sequences.

Overall, the war story anthology and native language dialogue deliver a WWII campaign with mechanical variety that persists beyond its technical shortcomings, creating an experience whose multiple perspectives justify attention despite persistent interface and stability problems.
