World War I trenches and a crumbling family mansion merge into a psychological horror landscape, where Paul von Schmidt navigates both physical danger and the weight of family dysfunction. The Unreal Engine 5 visuals establish an immediately unsettling atmosphere, with trench corridors and domestic interiors rendered in meticulous detail. From the opening moments, the dual setting between battlefield and home signals a narrative that prioritises psychological tension over jump scares.

Combat remains deliberately limited, with stealth and evasion forming the primary survival mechanics against blind monsters patrolling corridors. Environmental puzzles demand careful observation, from arranging candles and pendulums during a spiritual session to manipulating gas pressure valves in mechanical chambers. Chase sequences escalate tension through audio design, where proximity cues create the constant impression of pursuit. Collectible dog tags and documents scattered throughout each chapter flesh out the family's wartime history.

The spiritual session mechanic stands as the most distinctive puzzle sequence, requiring candle placement and pendulum manipulation to advance the narrative. Moral choices during boss encounters add mechanical variety, with options to play soothing music or harsh tones affecting the confrontation's outcome. The three-chapter structure, spanning Despair, Corruption, and Pain, maintains thematic coherence across shifting environments.

Gamma and brightness settings demand constant readjustment to spot collectibles in dimly lit areas, creating an unnecessary friction that pulls attention from the atmosphere. Mannequin sections introduce stealth challenges where enemy pathing becomes unpredictable, sometimes blocking narrow corridors without clear counterplay.

Overall, Ad Infinitum delivers a psychologically layered horror experience anchored in WWI family trauma, with environmental puzzle design and atmospheric detail carrying the experience past its technical shortcomings.
