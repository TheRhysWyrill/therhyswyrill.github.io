A globe-trotting task force chases a PETN-smuggling network from a Pakistan dock ambush through an Arabian Peninsula raid, a Bosnian arms-dealer manhunt, a Dubai pursuit and a Somalian cleric kill. The non-linear time-jumping structure baffles from the opening dock ambush onward, with side-switching and a training section set in a plane deepening the confusion, and the family framing lands with sincerity even when the chronology does not. A dual-perspective raid, played from both sides with a boat-bridge reveal that ties the halves together, is the one time-jump that fully works and the campaign's storytelling high-water mark, the sort of structural idea that deserved to carry the whole plot.

Mission variety is the genuine strength: stealth shore insertion, breach-and-clear with unlockable tomahawk and crowbar breaches, a controllable ground robot with unlimited grenades, a sniper section, two driving pursuits, one arcade-flavoured and one genuinely stealthy through Dubai traffic, a helicopter door-gunner sequence, and a final multi-breach compound assault. The Dubai stealth-driving sequence surprises by being genuinely cool, and the fighting robot is neat even if it handles like a worse version of a familiar sci-fi buggy. No two consecutive missions play the same way, and the side-switching structure means even the same raid arrives twice with different rules.

The Tier 1 realism framing keeps the tone grounded, with real smuggling inspiration behind the plot, and the wife's-eye-view epilogue is a closer few shooters attempt, echoing the earlier entry's closing message. The sincerity registers even through the structural fog. Character continuity from the prior game gives the ensemble a foundation, and the closer earns its sentiment without leaning on spectacle.

Enemy visibility is poor: muzzle flashes and damage indicators are often the only cues, with the shooting largely invisible until the bullets arrive. AI companions contribute little, standing by while the player does the shooting, and occasionally their deaths get reported as friendly fire for reasons that never become clear. The sniper section's bullet-drop mechanic is inscrutable, with the crosshair sometimes landing perfectly and sometimes demanding aim metres above the target, and it causes repeated failures without explanation.

Ammo is scarce throughout, making the PKP LMG the de facto crutch once found, and the final breach with overwhelming enemy numbers kills repeatedly, with enemies focusing the player exclusively and death arriving very quickly. The tomahawk unlock quietly contradicts its stealth promise, being anything but quiet, and the breach system never fully explains its own logic. AI deaths get flagged as friendly fire when hanging back rather than pushing forward, a scoring quirk that adds confusion to the read. The ending's replay demands are the campaign's most tiring stretch.

The whole campaign runs in a single sitting, short by the genre's standards and structured as a series of wild tonal swings that never quite cohere into a story but never stop being entertaining. Confusion about the plot is constant; enjoyment of the individual missions is nearly as constant, and the two coexist comfortably, a game easier to admire mission by mission than to follow as a whole.

Overall, a serious-toned military shooter whose mission variety carries a baffling time-jumped narrative, with invisible-enemy readability, passive AI allies and an inscrutable bullet-drop sniper wall as the real costs. A fun, short campaign that finally makes sense by the end, rewarding patience with a close that ties its halves together well.
