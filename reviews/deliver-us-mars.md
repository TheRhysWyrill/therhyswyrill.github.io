Deliver Us Mars delivers a strong narrative continuation that serves as a compelling follow-up to Deliver Us The Moon. The story effectively expands on the franchise lore and the personal history of the Johansson family through frequent, well-placed flashbacks that enrich the emotional stakes. This cinematic journey is supported by exceptional sound talent, featuring top-tier voice acting from notable industry veterans like Neil Newbon, which adds a high level of professional polish to the overall presentation.

The sequel introduces great mechanical upgrades, highlighted by improved character movement that feels significantly more grounded and responsive than the icy, roller-skate feel of the first game's protagonist. Exploration rewards you with breathtaking visual spectacles, showcasing impressive set pieces that range from detailed, advanced monolith arc vessels to diverse Martian landscapes, including unexpected snowy regions. Players will also encounter intuitive puzzle updates, as decrypting holograms and aligning stream point environmental puzzles feel much more streamlined and less tedious than the predecessor.

The experience is further enhanced by an engaging collectible system, making scannable objects and hidden Mars Man comics easier to find while providing meaningful flavor text that never feels like a chore. The technical execution is equally polished, offering smooth transitions from pre-rendered cutscenes to active gameplay alongside impactful technical details, like the uniquely exhausted visual look of the companion ASE units. However, this trip to the red planet still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from its central traversal mechanics and occasional technical glitches.

The gameplay suffers from inconsistent climbing mechanics, resulting in a central climbing system that is frequently frustrating and awkward to operate. Specific walls often completely fail to register your pickaxes despite having the correct visual texture, leading to accidental deaths and an annoying sense of unfairness. This issue is compounded by slow traversal and pacing issues, as character movement during these climbing sections is notably slow, and sequences like long elevator rides or extended walking segments can feel needlessly drawn out.

You will also encounter vague interactive feedback, where a lack of clear visual indicators makes it difficult to know exactly where a pickaxe will hook in or to identify subtle scannable objects in the environment. The narrative immersion is occasionally disrupted by repetitive dialogue and user interface prompts, causing philosophobot quotes and recurring tutorial screens to become irritating during serious story moments. Finally, exploration is limited by restrictive invisible walls that can be disorienting on open Martian plains, while disjointed chapter transitions and technical glitches like models getting stuck on geometry can mar the journey.

Overall Deliver Us Mars is a remarkably emotional, visually spectacular, and narratively rich sci-fi adventure that excels through its top-tier voice acting, responsive character movement, and streamlined puzzle design. While the Martian expedition is frequently slowed down by frustratingly finicky pickaxe climbing mechanics, slow traversal pacing, invisible boundaries, and vague interaction cues, the compelling family drama and fantastic environmental world-building ensure it stands as a highly satisfying sequel for fans of cinematic space mysteries.
