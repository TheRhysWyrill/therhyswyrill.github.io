Kathy Johnson joins Mission Opera to retrieve three colony ships that abandoned the moon base and landed on Mars. Her mother Claire leads the expedition, but the strained family dynamic overshadows the mission from the outset. Mars holds more than abandoned vessels; a struggling colony awaits on the red planet's surface. Answers about her father Isaac's disappearance drive Kathy forward through each discovery.

Environmental puzzles drive the core loop, with power conduit routing and object scanning providing variety between narrative beats. Each facility exploration segment rewards careful observation, and the Moonman comics add layered world-building. Voice performances, including Neil Newbon's recognition from Resident Evil 8, elevate the dialogue beyond functional delivery.

Character movement received a significant overhaul from Deliver Us The Moon, eliminating the "roller skates" feel that plagued the predecessor. Sprinting now requires holding the left trigger, friction that accumulates across extended sequences. Loading screens between sections occasionally interrupt pacing, though narrative momentum recovers quickly.

Power conduit puzzles demand spatial reasoning and circuit-tracing across multi-room facilities, each connection unlocking new areas. Environmental storytelling weaves through each abandoned structure, with holographic recordings and scattered documents reconstructing the colony's decline. The puzzle design avoids repetition by varying the challenge type across each act, preventing mechanical fatigue.

Climbing sections represent the weakest mechanical element, with the first wall displaying incorrect textures that suggest climbable surfaces where none exist. This visual feedback mismatch creates frustration during early platforming. Later climbing sections improve slightly with clearer visual cues, though the movement speed remains sluggish on vertical surfaces. Stealth encounters appear briefly near the conclusion, functional but underdeveloped compared to the exploration systems.

Overall, the emotional storytelling and improved movement mechanics deliver a science fiction adventure with real narrative weight, though the climbing system and texture misalignment prevent the experience from reaching its full potential.
