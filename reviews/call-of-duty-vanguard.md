Call of Duty: Vanguard delivers a stellar visual presentation and cinematic transitions, highlighting the campaign with smooth, highly polished shifts from cutscenes to active gameplay during the openings of Hamburg and Stalingrad. This cinematic flair is supported by exceptional weapon feedback and variety, offering punchy audio for firearms like the STG44 alongside visceral, crunchy sound design for close-quarters executions. The combat loop benefits from unique character abilities that keep the action engaging, giving you access to useful tactical features like aimbot-style focus precision and direct squad leader commands.

The mission design thrives on creative variety, keeping the pacing fresh by bouncing between a Rambo-style train hijacking, the high-stakes dive-bombing sequence at Midway, and the stealth-focused Lady Nightingale segments. Navigating these global theaters relies on satisfying atmospheric detail and immersion, treating you to realistic footprints in the snow and beautiful, painting-like lighting transitions. This historical journey is further enhanced by excellent voice acting, emotional flashback-heavy storytelling, and satisfying tactical depth that lets you use satchel charges to trigger glorious explosions against enemy fuel depots.

However, this World War II shooter still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from a frustrating initial user interface and structural narrative constraints. The experience suffers from a cluttered and god-awful user interface layout, drawing heavy criticism for utilizing the main menu to aggressively promote other titles. This structural friction is compounded by a repetitive campaign structure that relies far too heavily on flashbacks, leaving the present-day storyline feeling incredibly short and unbalanced.

The user experience introduces additional headaches through forced slow movement speed, making your character feel leisurely and sluggish during non-combat safe zones or narrative walking segments. You will also encounter severe technical performance issues, resulting in instances where the game crashes a lot or blue-screens your system to cause corrupted save files and lost progress. This technical frustration is worsened by visual artifacting and default blurriness, which introduces weird graphical distortions unless you manually fix the defaulted lower render resolution settings.

The tactical gameplay can occasionally stall due to artificial AI difficulty and hand-holding puzzle logic, introducing frustratingly spongy armored enemies alongside infuriating instant-kill guard dogs. Your immersion is further restricted by unhelpful companions who spoil objective directions far too quickly, removing any sense of independent discovery. Finally, the exploration elements falter due to poor collision detection and invisible walls, leaving you frequently stuck on minor environmental debris while trying to navigate the battlefield.

Overall Call of Duty: Vanguard is a remarkably cinematic, visually gorgeous, and mechanically diverse historical first-person shooter that excels through its seamless cutscene transitions, satisfying explosive combat, and varied character abilities. While the global campaign is occasionally bogged down by a bloated main menu interface, excessive flashback structures, severe technical system crashes, and sluggish narrative walking speeds, the outstanding audio design and thrilling set pieces ensure it stands as a highly entertaining and action-packed cinematic ride.
