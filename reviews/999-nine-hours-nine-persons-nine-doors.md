Nine strangers wake aboard a flooding ocean liner wearing numbered bracelets, with nine hours to find a door marked nine. Breaking the Nonary game's rules detonates a swallowed bomb, so every alliance forms under a hard countdown. The vessel is the Gigantic, a sister liner to a famous wreck, bought and restored by an obsessive collector, which dresses a Saw-style death game in Titanic-era fittings. A paranoid cast trades accusations while a voice-changed host called Zero issues instructions from hiding. Two presentation modes carry the mystery: a plain novel feed and a voice-acted adventure screen. The hook lands immediately, because every locked door advertises both an escape and a fragment of the larger design.

The cabin opener sets the template with a briefcase-and-dial puzzle that teaches searching before thinking. A casino follows, pairing poker slots with baccarat for a gambling-flavoured breather. The first-class cabin hides its exit behind a piano, and the laboratory surrenders its password to brute force. Chart-room routing and a telegraph puzzle then widen the toolkit from object hunts to signal reading. Each room escalates the vocabulary slightly, so solutions build on earlier tricks rather than restarting the education.

The captain's quarters pair a Morse cipher with a music box for the most musical lock in the chain. A freezer turns dry ice into a bottle bomb, and the operating room and chemical room trade keys and reagents in sequence. The torture-room experiment chair provides the grimmest set dressing without changing the puzzle grammar. A library book sort rewards systematic shelving, while the study multiplies small star-matching mini-games across its desks. Operating-room gurneys and chemical glassware keep the medical stretch visually distinct from the wooden panelled rooms behind it. Nothing here demands reflexes; patience and note-taking carry every lock.

The digital root governs every door: bracelets must sum to a door's root before it opens. Only three to five people may pass at a time, which turns group composition into a puzzle of its own. Authentication starts an 81-second countdown to reach the DEAD, compressing each crossing into a sprint of arithmetic. Swallowed bombs enforce the Nonary rules, so defiance carries an immediate physical price. The system stays legible throughout, and later doors remix the same sums with tighter parties rather than new mathematics.

Six endings branch from repeated choices, and a flowchart maps every route once seen. Selective skipping jumps past already-seen text, which keeps branch-hunting painless after the first ending. Later routes inherit knowledge from earlier ones, and morphic-field logic gives that inheritance an in-fiction explanation. Only one cipher, a base-36 conversion, truly stalls progress; the rest yield to steady poking. The true ending waits behind the full map, and reaching it reframes the routes that precede it.

The cast stays paranoid throughout, with accusations sharpening as the countdown narrows. Zero's announcements interrupt at fixed beats, keeping the death-game frame visible between escapes. Titanic-era fittings and collector lore give the liner more personality than its corridors strictly need. Twists compound in the back half, each new revelation arriving before the last finds its expositional footing. A late shift to Building Q relocates the search without resetting its rules.

Puzzle difficulty sits deliberately low across the whole chain. Brute force cracks the laboratory password, most locks yield to systematic searching, and only the base-36 conversion demands real working out. The resistance between story beats stays slight, so momentum rarely breaks on a puzzle. That softness suits the design, since the route map rather than any single lock provides the challenge.

Exposition repeats itself across routes, with the same explanations replayed each time a branch recrosses old ground. The true ending's wind-down stretches the circling talk to around forty minutes. An incinerator stretch drags hardest, talking at length where earlier sections let locks do the work. Flowchart locks compound the stall, gating branches without explaining the conditions. Together the padding and the opaque gates make the final hunt the slowest part of the design. The irritation stays structural rather than fatal, since skipping and the map keep progress moving.

Overall, this is a death-game mystery whose escape rooms and branching map sustain curiosity from the first bracelet check to the final reframe. Low puzzle resistance and a padded wind-down with opaque route locks cost momentum in the late stretch, but selective skipping and a legible door system keep the hunt moving. The conspiracy lands, the rooms satisfy, and the structure justifies its own replay demands.
