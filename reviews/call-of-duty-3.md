Call of Duty 3 delivers a surprisingly impressive visual clarity when upscaled through emulation to 1080p, transforming the original presentation into a clean and clear experience. This technical enhancement makes the subtitles and compass significantly easier to read, which allows you to appreciate realistic gunplay details like the rare ability to reload the M1 Garand mid-clip. The combat loop is further bolstered by satisfying melee attacks that carry aggressive force, sending enemy soldiers flying across the screen alongside rewarding hit markers that provide tactile feedback for successful shots.

The campaign thrives on unique mission variations and a strong environmental attention to detail, keeping the pacing engaging through diverse segments like driving a skinny Jeep named Vera and firing mortar shells at enemy supply trucks. Navigating these historical battlefields relies on an unexpectedly heavy and atmospheric musical score, at times channeling the high-intensity energy of modern action titles to elevate the tension. This military journey is rounded out by entertaining squad dynamics, treating you to corny yet iconic dialogue, surprising character development, and immersive touches like wine barrels that actively leak fluid when punctured by gunfire.

However, this classic World War II shooter still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from inconsistent AI behavior and gimmicky interaction mechanics. The single-player experience suffers from friendly squadmates who constantly run directly into your line of fire or block your paths, which combines with an enemy AI that unfairly singles you out or pops up instantly in cleared zones. This structural friction is compounded by awkward rowing mini-games and finicky prompt sequences like turning valves or unscrewing bombs, all of which rely on frustrating motion-style control logic.

The user experience introduces additional headaches through restricted movement speed and a dull visual palette, as the complete inability to sprint forces you into a sluggish snail's pace during narrative walks. You will also encounter artificial AI difficulty spikes, forcing you to deal with spongy armored enemy troops and an egregious frequency of incoming enemy grenades during late-game missions. This progression is further marred by inconsistent audio quality that exposes low-quality samples alongside a shaky camera and way too sensitive aiming controls on mounted weapons like the Pack 43.

Overall Call of Duty 3 is a remarkably varied, atmospherically heavy, and physically satisfying historical first-person shooter that shines through its impactful melee combat, rewarding emulation upscaling, and creative vehicular missions. While the frontline campaign is occasionally dragged down by frustratingly brain-dead ally AI, forced slow walking speeds, clunky motion mini-games, and a repetitive brown color scheme, the excellent squad banter and authentic weapon handling ensure it stands as an incredibly unique entry in the early franchise.
