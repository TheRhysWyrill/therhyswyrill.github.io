The car is the protagonist, and every system attaches to it: tyres, panels, battery, fuel and quirks all live on the vehicle, and the loop is built around driving into a junction, scavenging, fabricating upgrades in the garage and escaping before the instability storm closes in. Set on the Olympic Peninsula among scattered ARDA logs and a radio trio of companions, the campaign plays out as a thriller rather than the horror its reputation suggests, and the story beats land emotionally along the way. The core loop is more addictive than it has any right to be, pulling a full run through to the ending.

Anomaly chaos is the highlight. Unpredictable environmental hazards create genuinely memorable moments: a glittering boulder accident can launch the car at highway speed, and if an air strip happens to be nearby, the flight covers half the map. Bouncing rabbit anomalies yank the car skyward for no reason, fog turns eerily beautiful, and weather reshapes every junction's mood. Atmosphere is weather-driven rather than horror-driven, which suits the material: eerie when foggy, tranquil when clear, never quite scary, and none the worse for it.

The garage fabricator drives progression. Tyres, panels, armour, backpack upgrades and car modules unlock in tiers gated by scanned anomalies and rare materials: swamp coral, limb magnets and Olympian fragments feed the deeper recipes. Weather- and tyre-dependent handling delights in retrospect, spinning and flipping in mud, sliding in rain, but caused real frustration mid-run before the right tyre compounds were found. Old-wall and barricade set-piece crossings add linear mission variety to the procedural structure, and the deep zone raises tension sharply in the final act.

Friction lives around the loop rather than in it. All-terrain tyres are excellent and pop constantly, fuel drain quirks on certain junctions punish route planning, and water stalls engines at the worst moments. The 141-slot inventory with awkward storage and a clunky pickup interface draws repeated small gripes, and the RNG is the biggest structural complaint: text logs, audio logs, anomalies and crafting components all spawn randomly, so a full clear is effectively impossible, and unscannable rare components can lock recipes away entirely. One locked limb magnet meant the magnetic hammer and everything downstream of it never got made.

Junction rerolls enable endless play after the story but actively kill completionism, since the map rerolls at the start of every run and nothing found is guaranteed to reappear. That trade is a deliberate design choice, and it works for story-focused runs while fighting anyone who wants a fixed world to fully conquer. The first-session mistake of clearing every junction added hours that a more targeted approach would have skipped, which says more about the loop's addictive pull than any design flaw.

The post-game expeditions are skippable without guilt: the story is the campaign, and the endless mode is there for those who want the loop without the ending. The radio trio carries the narrative warmth, and one character's death in the old-wall crossing lands as the emotional peak, echoed poignantly at the ending. The tone shift from expected horror to thriller is the game's most honest surprise, and the material is stronger for it.

Overall, this is a distinctly original driving-survival hybrid whose anomaly chaos, car-centric loop and weather-driven atmosphere outweigh popping tyres, clunky inventory friction and RNG that locks collectibles and recipes away. Both thrilling and chill in the same breath, and easy to recommend on the strength of its systems.
