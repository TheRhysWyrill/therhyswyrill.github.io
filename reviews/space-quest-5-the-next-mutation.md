Space Quest 5 graduates its hero from janitor to captain, handing over the SCS Eureka, a garbage scow, and a bridge-simulator cold open that announces the Star Trek parody from the first frame. The satire never relents: satirical aptitude-test questions, a Vulcan-ish rival captain, and a running phone-call-from-Sprint gag woven into a space station jailbreak. Voice acting from the previous entry is gone, shifting the delivery burden to narration, and the goofy theme music sets the register immediately. The tone is broader and more sitcom-flavoured than the series' surreal entries, and the joke density sustains it. What elevates the game beyond its parody shell is the crew.

The ensemble is the game's constant delight. Cliffy, the hapless engineer whose repairs amount to smacking things, is the standout, but the whole roster earns its place: man-hungry com officer Flo, taciturn Droole, and eventually WD40, a reprogrammed assassin android joining the roster. Spike, the acid-piddling pet adopted mid-campaign, becomes an endearing running thread. The crew-intercom system, switching between crew members to solve problems, gives the game a management layer no other entry in the series had, and the banter between them is the writing's best material.

The Battle Cruiser duel against Captain Quirk lands as the surprise hit. Played as Battleship in space, the minigame trades puzzle logic for position reads and hidden ships, and it delivers the campaign's most satisfying head-to-head. It comes with a caveat: winning leans partly on luck, and a near-perfect end-of-game score is achievable everywhere except this duel. As a set piece, though, it earns its place.

Icon-driven point-and-click conventions run underneath a starship command layer of laying in courses, running garbage-collection beacons and shuttling between destinations. Copy-protection coordinate charts return as series tradition, handled smoothly enough with the manual at hand. Shuttle missions to Clorox 2 pack mutant shootouts, a colonist's log and a Genetics Research canister clue, while the Thrakus escape-pod rescue extends the running thread of finally meeting Beatrice Wankmeister. The command layer gives the campaign structure that pure screen-to-screen puzzling would lack.

The EVA pod Cliffy rescue is the friction peak. Timed fuel management, repeated fuel-deaths and finicky grappling make the sequence the game's loudest annoyance, and the grabbing mechanic refuses to cooperate exactly when precision matters. The Goliath air-duct climb compounds the frustration through sheer repetition, demanding the same crawl far more times than the joke warrants. Both sequences are signposted well enough to finish, but neither is fun to control.

Signposting wobbles elsewhere too. The space-bar monkey disaster and station escape descend into chaos rather than clear direction, leaving destruction to be stumbled through rather than solved. The WD40 chase on Kiz Urazgubi, by contrast, supplies light tension without real frustration, a well-tuned needle the rest of the action sequences miss. The Goliath infiltration itself, ducts and all, resolves into a stealth ship-board finale that ends with vacuuming the rival's blob into the trash compactor, a punchline that justifies the climb.

Overall, Space Quest 5 is one of the series' most likeable entries, carried by ensemble chemistry and goofy warmth rather than puzzle innovation. The EVA rescue and duct climbs are genuinely awkward, and the Battle Cruiser leans on luck, yet the parody lands and the crew makes the whole campaign feel inhabited. As a highlight of the saga it holds up.
