Playing an ordinary trooper rather than a Spartan changes everything. Death comes easily, the health model is renamed stamina and recharges slower, and every enemy that a supersoldier shrugs off becomes a genuine threat to a soldier in body armour rather than powered exoskeleton. A squad separated mid-drop in a rainy occupied city at night supplies the premise, and the noir mood, subdued score and film-styled presentation make this the series' most atmospheric and structurally distinctive entry. It is a change of pace that still plays like Halo.

The Rookie's open-city hub is the structural idea that makes the game. Drop pods scatter, the city is dark and quiet, and piecing together the squad's night through crash-site clues and audio logs gives exploration genuine purpose. Beacons, subways and progressively unlocking routes shape the hub, with supply caches rewarding thorough searching by unlocking the silenced SMG, sniper and pistol. The flashback structure, playing each squadmate's mission then catching up as the Rookie, is inventive but disjointed, loose enough that two missions can be played out of order without the game flagging it.

Sadie's Story is the audio drama that outshines everything else in the collectible space. Threading a civilian tale of a superintendent AI, a father and a corrupt commissioner through the city's logs, it hooks hard enough to want resolution, and the noir score beneath it turns the hub exploration into something approaching a radio play. The squad's banter, Buck, Romeo, Dutch, Mickey and Dare trading insults between drop and reunion, gives the cast their charm, and the writing earns its jokes.

Set-pieces span the Kikowani tunnel flight, NMPD rooftop sniping, ONI Alpha Site's bridge demolition and the Coastal Highway Warthog and Scorpion escort finale, and the Kikowani Phantom ride in particular earns its "best mission" billing. The Dutch level, starting with a Spartan laser against Wraiths and Choppers, is the only genuine difficulty spike, and the laser itself is the arsenal's standout, with the silenced pistol's headshot power a close second. The standard pistol is not even remotely viable once the silenced variant arrives.

Friction comes from the allies more than the enemies. AI drivers undermine the vehicle set-pieces repeatedly, wandering the longest way to a driver's seat or steering into danger, and the enemies feel disproportionately tanky against the trooper's fragility. The missing sprint, toggle-crouch and dual wielding of the earlier games make the sandbox feel sparser than its siblings, and the MCC enhanced mode introduces dithered, flickering textures on characters and trees, quirks that the classic mode avoids.

Around four hours covers the campaign, notably shorter and less brutal than memory suggests, with only a level or two genuinely punishing. The six-hour gap at the story's centre, the Rookie's missing night, stays as mysterious as the framing intends, and the squad's phantom escape closes the arc cleanly. The drop intro remains one of the series' great openers, all confidence and music, and it sets the tone the whole game sustains. The jazzy calm between firefights remains jarring against the combat for some tastes, but the restraint is the point: no supersoldier spectacle, just a soldier trying to regroup.

Overall, this is a distinctive, atmospheric detour whose human-scale noir structure, Sadie's Story and squad banter outweigh useless AI drivers, tanky enemies and a few enhanced-mode visual glitches. Shorter and easier than legend suggests, and well worth the detour.
