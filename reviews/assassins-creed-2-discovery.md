Ezio Auditore travels to Spain between memories of the main game, where protecting Christopher Columbus from Templar interference unfolds through 2D side-scrolling platforming. The premise carries the franchise's established narrative weight onto handheld hardware, with the Spanish Inquisition providing historical context for the conspiracy. From the opening moments, the smoother performance compared to Altaïr's Chronicles communicates genuine technical improvement. Full voice acting, extremely rare for the Nintendo DS, brings the story to life with unexpected fidelity.

Core gameplay translates Assassin's Creed's core mechanics into a side-scrolling format, with climbing, parkour, and assassination mechanics adapted for 2D traversal. Hiding spots and stealth sections mirror the console titles, though detection feels inconsistent and finicky during later missions. The movement speed proves problematic relative to the field of view, causing visibility issues during precision platforming and stealth sequences. Mission variety spans infiltration, chase sequences, and combat encounters across the campaign.

The full voice acting stands as the most distinctive technical achievement, delivering character dialogue that elevates the narrative beyond typical DS limitations. The 2D side-scrolling interpretation of Assassin's Creed mechanics creates a unique handheld experience that translates franchise concepts effectively. Performance improvements over Altaïr's Chronicles demonstrate genuine technical progress for the platform.

Stealth missions become frustrating due to finicky detection systems and inconsistent assassination animations that create unreliable outcomes. Later levels introduce difficulty spikes through checkpoint distances and platforming precision that clash with the movement speed. Some text corruption appears during emulation, likely an emulation issue rather than a game defect.

Overall, Assassin's Creed II: Discovery delivers a technically impressive handheld interpretation through voice acting and performance improvements, though stealth mechanics and later level design create enough friction to complicate a straightforward recommendation.
