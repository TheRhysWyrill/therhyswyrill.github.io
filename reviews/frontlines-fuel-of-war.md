Frontlines: Fuel of War delivers a spectacularly pleasant surprise on Linux, showcasing flawless out of the box performance on SteamOS using older Proton versions to clean up the jittery cutscenes encountered on Windows hardware. This technical stability is beautifully enhanced by automatic forced VSync handling, treating you to a clean presentation that entirely eliminates severe screen tearing issues. The war campaign keeps the pacing consistently engaging through a better musical score than expected, combining beautifully with an expendable grunt redeployment system that creates an immense feeling of large scale military conflict.

The core gameplay loop thrives on explosive vehicle missions and unique gadget utilization, keeping combat thrilling through a continuous sprint mechanic that features absolutely no stamina limits across massive maps. Navigating these active frontlines relies on piloting a cool remote-controlled helicopter equipped with rockets, destroying enemy positions with fast-firing tanks, and landing satisfying sniper headshots that send hostile helmets flying. This tactical warfare features impressive environmental terraforming details, letting you witness real-time terrain destruction while occasionally cheating infantry only zones by creatively wedging your armored vehicles into tight corridors.

However, this classic military shooter still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from broken controller button layouts and an complete lack of native menu glyphs. The single-player experience suffers from native gamepad support that stubbornly refuses to show correct button prompts, forcing you to constantly guess your inputs during high stakes fire fights. This mechanical layout is further worsened by odd analog stick sensitivities that feel incredibly sluggish, alongside completely broken controller mappings that prevent you from using vital crouch and prone functions.

The user experience introduces additional headaches through unfair enemy scaling and pinpoint artificial intelligence accuracy. You will encounter frustrating titanium enemies who easily absorb an entire magazine of ammunition while your own character feels like they are made of papermâché. This progression is made more irritating by hostile snipers who possess near-perfect accuracy from extreme distances even while moving, which combines poorly with insane weapon recoil on your own light machine guns and a brain dead friendly AI pathfinding system that constantly blocks your vehicles.

The tactical progression can occasionally stall due to a sparse primary weapon selection and intrusive tutorial prompts. The weapon variety feels far too limited over time, leaving you with only a few effective assault rifles to utilize across the entire campaign. Finally, the story draws to a highly disappointing finish, leaving you deeply unsatisfied by a weird cliffhanger ending that clearly set up a sequel that never actually arrived.

Overall Frontlines: Fuel of War is a remarkably smooth, acoustically impressive, and mechanically unique military shooter on Linux that excels through its explosive drone gadgets, satisfying tank combat, and infinite sprinting traversal freedom. While the massive battlefield is heavily restricted by broken controller mappings, missing menu icons, bullet sponge enemy health pools, and a sudden cliffhanger resolution, the outstanding technical performance on modern handheld software and chaotic large-scale grunt mechanics ensure it stands as a highly entertaining and triumphant retro gem.
