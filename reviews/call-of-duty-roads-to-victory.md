Multiple Allied campaigns span Italy, Normandy, and the Netherlands, with the game exceeding expectations for a PSP shooter through competent design choices that adapt the franchise's formula to handheld hardware. The atmosphere is action-focused with squad banter adding personality, maintaining engagement despite the platform's technical constraints.

The auto-aim system proves crucial for making the PSP's single-analogue control scheme work, providing responsive targeting that transforms potentially frustrating firefights into satisfying encounters. This mechanical adaptation demonstrates thoughtful design that prioritises playability over the precision demanded by dual-analogue setups.

Mission variety impresses with infantry combat, plane gunnery sequences, and defensive objectives that break the standard shooting formula. The different mission types provide pacing diversity, with each sequence introducing distinct combat scenarios that prevent repetition across the campaign's structure.

Regenerating health and generous checkpoints maintain accessibility, making the experience approachable despite the PSP's control limitations. The difficulty balance feels fair, with the auto-aim compensating for the hardware's input constraints while still providing satisfying challenge during intense firefights.

The control scheme remains inherently limited by PSP hardware, with no second analogue stick for camera control creating awkward movement during multi-directional engagements. The lack of a jump button restricts verticality, though the auto-aim system mitigates these limitations during most combat encounters.

Friendly AI occasionally pushes the player into enemy fire, creating frustrating deaths that feel unavoidable rather than challenging. This collision detection issue proves minor but persistent, particularly during sequences where squad members cluster in narrow corridors.

Overall, the auto-aim system and mission variety establish a surprisingly competent handheld FPS that proves the PSP could handle the genre effectively, though hardware limitations create control constraints that prevent the experience from reaching its full potential.
