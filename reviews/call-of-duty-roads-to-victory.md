Call of Duty: Roads to Victory delivers generous modern quality-of-life features, including regenerating health, frequent checkpoints, and a reliable aim-assist target-lock system that makes the action accessible and smooth. This solid baseline supports impressive visual performance, offering well-preserved textures and environments that successfully capture a cohesive World War II atmosphere. The experience is elevated by varied campaign perspectives, allowing you to play through distinct missions as American, Canadian, and British forces to provide a great sense of scope.

The core gameplay loop thrives on satisfying combat feedback, utilizing visceral hit markers and clear audio cues to help mitigate the challenges of navigating a handheld shooter scheme. Progressing through the front lines introduces creative gimmick missions, most notably a thrilling B-17 bomber level that breaks up standard infantry combat by requiring you to manage multiple gunner stations and on-board emergencies. This action benefits from a tactile weapon variety, ensuring that classic firearms like the Sten, MP44, and M1A1 Carbine feel completely distinct with satisfying reload animations and heavy mechanical sound effects.

However, this portable military shooter still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from inherent handheld control limitations and restrictive environmental tracking. The gameplay suffers from clunky aiming mechanics due to the original hardware lacking a second analog stick, which creates imprecise movement that can feel awkward during intense firefights. This navigation friction is compounded by a total lack of a sprint mechanic, making character movement feel sluggish when crossing larger open fields, while the inability to jump or climb over basic obstacles creates artificial barriers in the level design.

The user experience introduces additional frustration through opaque and intrusive invisible walls, where oversized hitboxes on trees and rubble frequently block your path or push your soldier directly into incoming enemy fire. You will also encounter unpredictable enemy spawning, as German soldiers occasionally pop into existence or completely despawn right in front of you to cause unfair damage. The historical journey is further restricted by a frustratingly short campaign length, wrapping up all fourteen levels across three countries in roughly three hours.

Finally, the technical execution can encounter a few stability issues, suffering from repetitive technical glitches and occasional hard locks during transitions between major campaign segments. The storytelling also feels a bit stripped down, relying on an underwhelming narrative presentation that uses static images instead of cinematic cutscenes to explain mission context. When emulated, however, the overall presentation is significantly saved by the effective use of high-quality filters that keep the text and user interface sharp and legible.

Overall Call of Duty: Roads to Victory is an impressive, visually atmospheric, and action-packed handheld shooter that excels through its diverse campaign perspectives, satisfying firearm feedback, and creative vehicle missions. While the wartime journey is occasionally slowed down by clunky hardware control limitations, a short three-hour runtime, restrictive invisible boundaries, and sudden enemy pop-in glitches, the generous checkpoint system and strong core combat mechanics ensure it stands as a remarkably competent and nostalgic portable military experience.
