Maxwell and Lily drop into the DC universe with a notebook that turns any written word into a real object, and their scattered Starites pull them across Gotham, Metropolis, OA, Central City, Atlantis and Paradise Island. A dry-voiced narrator bookends the tour, and a mysterious Doppelganger recruits one villain after another as the arc escalates from street-level crime to multiverse scale. The superhero framing gives this entry a hook its predecessor never had, and the escalation keeps the tour moving forward.

Object creation remains the star. Any noun spawns into the world and adjectives stack freely on top, producing jetpacks, floaty powers, mirror shields against petrifying gazes, tin-foil hats for nanobot waves, and disguise mustaches for visiting royalty. The Batcomputer supplies character bios and costumes, playable team-ups span Batgirl, Robin, Cyborg, Green Lantern, Flash, Aquaman and Wonder Woman, and unlockable origin stories give each hero a playable vignette that no other entry in the series offers.

Boss fights split between puzzle and brawl. Making Metallo weak, clueless and ridable solves him without a punch thrown, reflecting a laser back with a mirror ends another duel cleanly, and stealing a trident with a magnet handles an ocean throne's occupant with style. The final gauntlet against Brainiac summons alternate-universe Justice League variants for spectacle, but adjectives get stripped repeatedly, waves drag, and deaths force the whole buff set-up to be redone.

The randomly generated task pool is the structural compromise. Solutions repeat, steal, shrink, make portable, or summon the League, and the endless supply trades the predecessor's curated, area-specific puzzles for quantity. Difficulty barely registers either: reputation flows so freely that the system penalising easy solutions never actually bites, and most objectives collapse under a two-word shortcut. Traversal trivialises itself early as well, with floaty and flying self-buffs rendering every vertical challenge optional, and the self-imposed discipline of avoiding them becomes the only real difficulty modifier. The sandbox rewards creativity only as long as the player brings their own.

World variety partly compensates for the shallow tasks. Gotham's street crime, Atlantis's throne-room politics, OA's cosmic scale and Paradise Island's mythology each carry distinct visual identities and era-appropriate gimmicks, and the tour structure gives each destination a reason to show off. A Watchtower mission, a Fortress of Solitude visit and the closing ship-board gauntlet punctuate the area rotation with set pieces. The Batcomputer's bios reward curiosity with decades of character lore, and costume unlocks dress the duo in the whole catalogue. As a guided tour of a comics universe, the pacing is genuinely efficient.

Controls and camera rough the edges. Interactions misfire when clicking objects, the camera zooms unexpectedly, drifts off Maxwell during fights and never locks on reliably, and occasional lockups interrupt otherwise smooth play without a clear cause. Spelling friction remains the series' signature hazard, costing attempts on batarangs, sledgehammers, sandals of mythological speed and the occasional heavenly noun.

Overall, the unrestricted creation system inside a licensed superhero universe delivers genuine delight, and the DC setting, playable origin stories and puzzle-shaped bosses give it charm worth recommending. Shallow randomised tasks, control jank and a temperamental camera keep it a clear step beneath its predecessor, and the spongy finale tests patience. A likeable, uneven sandbox whose best ideas still land.
