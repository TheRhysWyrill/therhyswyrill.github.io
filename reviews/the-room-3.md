The Room Three is the biggest entry in the series, spreading its puzzle boxes across a lighthouse, a clock tower, a forge and an observatory. The craftsman's letters set the task of recovering four pieces of a key to the null element and bringing them to a central font.

The four-and-a-half-hour runtime is roughly double that of its predecessors, and the extra scale suits the material. Reaching every conclusion requires revisiting Greyhome through a change-your-fate option that keeps progress, so replays are quick.

Four endings branch from the same point, gated by hidden artifacts and a fortune teller's tokens, each reworking the established tools. The final route combines both towers and all three light beams, and the closing riddle about mazes and labyrinths lands the theme.

The forge chapter stands out, with keys crafted from gold ingots and a single multi-purpose key reused across several locks. The observatory's constellation puzzles and the real-time clock puzzle that waits for a specific hour add welcome variety.

The horror coating is stronger than in earlier entries, with jump scares and a creeping atmosphere running through the house. The library's fortune teller and the craftsman's notes carry a deeper narrative between the mechanisms.

The camera and rotation controls remain the weak spot, constantly swinging perspective during sliding puzzles that demand precision. The ball-maze and knight-move chest puzzles are highlights, while sliding-block arrangements draw the most frustration.

Overall, puzzle design reaches its richest form here, and the multiple endings give the game real staying power. No entry in the series has been bad, and this one is the best value for the time.
