Greedy Pea embarks on a coin-collecting sprint through 25 compact stages, each designed for repeated attempts rather than extended exploration. The Game Boy-inspired green palette and chiptune soundtrack establish an immediate retro identity, while the core loop of dodging hazards and gathering collectibles delivers tight, replayable challenges across themed worlds.

Every level pairs a distinct hazard layout with bite-sized spacing, encouraging another go rather than punishment for failure. Coins and diamonds serve as both score targets and navigation markers, guiding movement toward optional secrets. Themed worlds shift between forests, caves, castles, and trains, maintaining visual variety within the constrained colour scheme.

Pursuing full completion transforms casual platforming into a precision test. Collecting every coin and gem in a level demands tighter routing and more deliberate timing, raising the difficulty ceiling considerably. Bubble hazards, however, prove especially aggravating: their hitboxes feel oversized and unpredictable, creating deaths that feel arbitrary rather than earned.

The double jump mechanic is central but carries a critical limitation: it only activates after an initial jump, not when falling from ledges. This restriction leads to unexpected deaths during edge navigation, where the expected safety net simply does not appear. Saw blades appear in nearly every stage, becoming a predictable but persistent presence that rewards pattern recognition.

Coin count displays occasionally show incorrect totals, causing confusion when pursuing 100% completion in levels where the displayed number does not match the actual collectible count. The CRT visual filter, while atmospheric, offers fewer configuration options than some might prefer.

Overall, the bite-sized level design and completionist depth deliver a compact platformer with mechanical substance that persists, even if the bubble hitboxes and limited double jump introduce friction that disrupts the otherwise tight precision.
