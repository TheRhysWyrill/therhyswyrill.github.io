Call of Duty: Black Ops III delivers an impressively beefy campaign length that clocks in at nearly seven hours, making the experience feel significantly more substantial than other entries in the series. This lengthy ride is supported by excellent visual fidelity and an outstanding futuristic aesthetic, showcasing beautiful graphical quality alongside awesome animated weapon skins like Dragon Fire and Cosmic. The overall presentation hits all the right notes with high intensity, treating you to smooth cinematic transitions that seamlessly blend pre-rendered cutscenes directly into active gameplay.

The core gameplay loop thrives on diverse cybernetic core abilities, giving you awesome tactical tools like Fireflies, Immolation, and Remote Hijack to expertly control large groups of enemies. Navigating this mind-bending sci-fi narrative reveals highly intriguing world-building, taking you through creative concepts like the Direct Neural Interface, the Frozen Forest, and a bizarre World War II fever dream sequence. Combat remains incredibly satisfying thanks to the heavy feel of light machine guns, a handy spike launcher, and a tactical mode that brilliantly highlights enemy positions and incoming grenades.

However, this ambitious shooter still faces a few notable hurdles stemming from restricted weapon scavenging and inconsistent movement mechanics. The campaign experience suffers from a strange limitation that completely prevents you from picking up weapons dropped by fallen enemies, forcing you to remain strictly locked into your pre-chosen loadout. This tactical friction is compounded by a finicky wall-running system that frequently fails to activate despite correct inputs, turning high-speed traversal segments into a frustrating chore.

The narrative pacing introduces additional headaches through the overuse of scripted dialogue, driving the repetitive explanation of events like train go boom from a funny meme into an intensely grating annoyance. You will also encounter artificial AI spawning and sudden difficulty spikes, resulting in drones and rogue robots that continually spawn right on top of you in ways that feel entirely unfair. This structural layout is further restricted by forced slow movement speeds during safe zone narrative interludes, artificially reducing your pace to a sluggish and leisurely walk.

The gameplay can occasionally experience minor friction from technical glitches and awkward user interface nuances, leading to jittery character animations on moving elevators and confusing prompts when climbing specific debris. Your independent discovery is further dampened by hand-holding companions who spoil puzzle directions far too quickly, alongside ambiguous control triggers that can cause unintended deaths during high-stakes escape sequences. Despite these mechanical optimization hurdles, the memorable encounters with giant mechs, mother ships, and creepy rogue robots provide a solid challenge.

Overall Call of Duty: Black Ops III is a remarkably lengthy, visually gorgeous, and mechanically creative sci-fi first-person shooter that excels through its diverse cybernetic battlefield abilities, mind-bending story sequences, and satisfying heavy gunplay. While the futuristic campaign is occasionally dragged down by the baffling inability to scavenge enemy weapons, inconsistent wall-running physics, repetitive dialogue writing, and unfair enemy drone spawns, the exceptional combat depth and memorable boss designs ensure it stands as a highly entertaining and uniquely ambitious entry in the franchise.
