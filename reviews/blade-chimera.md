Shin, a Venator demon hunter, awakens from thirty years of cryogenic stasis to bond with Lux, a luminina demon who serves as both weapon and companion. The premise unfolds across post-apocalyptic Japan where demons have emerged for decades under Holy Union control. A tense dystopian backdrop rewards exploration across interconnected zones hiding secrets and upgrades.

Combat centres on the dual weapon system, where Lux functions as sword, healing tool, and source of special attacks alongside conventional guns, whips, and shurikens. Switching between Lux and other weapons creates a rhythm where health drops from Lux strikes and MP accumulates from weapon attacks. The skill tree adds progression depth, unlocking new abilities that expand traversal and combat options.

Fast travel becomes available from the opening hours, a quality-of-life choice that eliminates tedious backtracking across interconnected zones. Save points double as teleport nodes, allowing instant movement between discovered areas. This accessibility means side quests and collectible hunting feel optional rather than mandatory, with puzzle pieces tucked into hidden corners rewarding curiosity.

The clearance level system gates access to new regions through tiers one to four, requiring specific upgrades before certain paths open. Environmental storytelling emerges through the Peacemaker autonomous zone, a contrasting refuge from the Holy Union's authoritarian grip where the dystopian atmosphere blends sci-fi and supernatural elements.

Character animations maintain exceptional fluidity throughout, with Shin's movement reading cleanly during fast-paced combat. The default control scheme, however, maps restoring objects to the same button used for Lux's weapon functions, creating unintended interactions during heated encounters and awkward menu navigation.

Overall, the smooth animations and immediate fast travel deliver a Metroidvania with polished traversal and combat variety, though the control scheme and a punishing final encounter prevent the experience from maintaining its momentum throughout.
