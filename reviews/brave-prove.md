Directional inputs drive combat in this 1998 PlayStation action RPG where Oz and the Wild Things search for the Black Crystal across the land of Milfala. The fighting game-style combo system demands specific button sequences rather than simple button mashing, creating depth unusual for the era. Elemental powers unlock new traversal and combat options as dungeons are cleared.

The Water, Earth, Fire, Wind, and Chaos temples each demand different elemental abilities, with wind power granting aerial mobility that transforms navigation. Elemental souls collected from defeated enemies feed into a progression system that strengthens Oz's sword and abilities. Music accompanies each temple with distinct tracks that reinforce environmental themes.

Dungeon complexity escalates through maze-like layouts where corridors and rooms repeat visually, demanding careful map navigation or backtracking through identical corridors. Enemies resist damage during stunned states, requiring sustained assault rather than exploiting vulnerability windows. This mechanic persists throughout, creating drawn-out encounters even against basic foes.

Boss fights restrict menu access, preventing equipment changes or item usage mid-combat. This limitation proves frustrating when damage output proves insufficient against later bosses, particularly the final encounter that demands sustained effort without adaptation. Free sword upgrades between dungeons provide meaningful power increases, though progression feels uneven.

Sprint activation requires double-tapping the analog stick, creating awkward movement during exploration. The absence of invincibility frames allows extended hitstun chains that trap Oz in prolonged damage sequences. These control quirks layer onto the already challenging combat, creating friction that persists across the adventure.

Overall, the directional combo system and elemental soul progression deliver an action RPG with mechanical depth, though maze-like dungeons and boss fight restrictions create frustration that tests persistence beyond enjoyment.
