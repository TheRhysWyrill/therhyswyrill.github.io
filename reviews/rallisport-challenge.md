Rally stages and circuit races combine in this 2002 racer that blends two distinct racing disciplines into a cohesive package. Visual fidelity remains impressive for its era, with detailed environments across diverse locations that hold up remarkably well. The career structure spans four difficulty tiers, each unlocking new cars and tracks progressively.

Different cars handle distinctly across tarmac, gravel, mud, and ice surfaces, creating varied driving experiences. The Peugeot 206 proves particularly responsive, while other vehicles feel stiff and unresponsive during aggressive manoeuvres. Surface types drastically alter handling characteristics, requiring careful adaptation between surface conditions.

Camera options prove limited and sometimes obstructive during intense racing sequences. The co-driver provides pace notes that many drivers ignore due to unclear delivery. Bonus events recycle existing tracks rather than introducing fresh content.

Tarmac handling on certain cars creates satisfying high-speed moments. The Mitsubishi Lancer Evolution delivers precise steering response on paved surfaces. These moments contrast sharply with the frustrating stiffness experienced on other vehicles.

Mud and ice sections demand careful throttle control with stiff-handling vehicles. The difficulty spikes dramatically during unlimited-tier events, creating frustration. Damage remains cosmetic, with no performance degradation from collisions.

The career progression rewards consistency across rally and circuit events. Point-based advancement encourages replaying earlier tiers for better results. The 29-car roster provides variety despite uneven handling quality.

Overall, the rally and circuit race split delivers varied racing with exceptional 2002 visuals and satisfying surface-type dynamics. Stiff vehicle handling and a punishing late-game difficulty curve create friction, but the varied racing disciplines sustain engagement throughout.
