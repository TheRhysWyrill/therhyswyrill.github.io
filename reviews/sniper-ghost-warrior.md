A sniper-only loadout, rifle and silenced pistol with no assault-rifle backup, forces long-range discipline in a way the series' later entries abandoned. Levels alternate between concealed overwatch positions and close-quarters assault sections, and only the former fit the design. The still-sniping stretches, picking off marked targets from hidden spots with breath control and patience, carry the game's identity, and the bullet cams turn each successful shot into theatre, helmets and hats flying off on impact. When the loop works, the discipline of choosing a position, marking a target and executing without discovery delivers exactly what the premise promises.

The visibility meter is a genuinely useful detection system once understood, filling white for suspicion and red for a confirmed spot, and enemy markers placed on the map give blind navigation a fair aid. A heartbeat and stamina layer feeds the tension of held breath before a shot, and limited syringe healing adds consequence to mistakes. The spotter-role twist of the Guardian Angel mission, playing the observer who calls shots rather than the shooter, is a genuinely novel angle for the genre even when the mission's scripting collapses around it.

Bullet physics are inconsistent enough to undermine the core. Enemies shrug off chest shots, helmets fly off without kills, and rounds occasionally vanish into invisible walls, failures that strike at the heart of a game built entirely on ballistics. Difficulty tuning compounds the problem from the other side: AI is deaf to nearby gunfire yet laser-accurate at extreme range, and the moment a shot lands everyone on the map knows exactly where the shooter lies, an old-shooter detection pattern this entry inherits wholesale.

Buggy AI states force restarts. The Guardian Angel mission bugged into unwinnable full-alert chases requiring repeated retries, and a uranium-mine support mission stopped registering bullets entirely until a checkpoint reset. Broken scripting in a sniping game is particularly costly, since a single discovered position costs the whole infiltration, and the restart loop eats more patience than any designed challenge does.

Ammunition generosity defuses the tension the design wants. Two hundred and fifty to three hundred sniper rounds handed out per section remove any scarcity the stealth loop should carry, and AI allies repeat the same lines on loop at full volume, positioning themselves acoustically right next to the player no matter the actual distance. The narrative is thin and stitched together, perspective shifts between sniper and assault soldier arrive unexplained, and the campaign closes on a final shot followed immediately by an end card with no denouement, the jarring abruptness epitomising the disjointed whole.

Navigation and movement friction fill the margins. Rope climbing feels unreliable, crouched stair-climbing glitches constantly, sprint activation carries a strange delay, and the absence of vaulting and an accessible map strands the player on navigation puzzles, including a long walk back to an extraction point with no way to check the route. The Undercover of Night downloadable chapter hammers a single trap failure repeatedly without variation.

Overall, the still-sniping core, the bullet-cam theatre and the visibility meter deliver genuine satisfaction in patches, while buggy AI, inconsistent ballistics, looping ally chatter and a disjointed structure drag the rest down to average. The series improved substantially with its later entries, and this origin shows its age in every system outside the scope.
