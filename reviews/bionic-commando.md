Bionic Commando drops Nathan Spencer, a bionic commando released from death row, into Ascension City to investigate a terrorist attack. The bionic arm immediately distinguishes the experience: grappling onto buildings, swinging between structures, and launching death-from-above attacks create a movement system that blends Spider-Man-style traversal with military shooter combat. From the opening moments, the arm's physics demand timing and spatial awareness that reward mastery.

Between missions, the city's vertical architecture encourages exploration through creative swinging routes. Destructible environments and interactive objects provide opportunities for improvised combat approaches, with the arm ripping apart structures and hurling debris at enemies. Weapon variety, spanning pistols, SMGs, and grenade launchers, supplements the arm's versatility without overshadowing its centrality.

The targeting system, however, creates friction: the arm's ability to grapple onto nearly everything makes locking onto specific targets inconsistent, especially during encounters with multiple enemies. Late-game sections compound this by flooding corridors with enemies, overwhelming the targeting system's limitations and reducing encounters to bullet-sponge attrition rather than tactical engagements.

Boss fights punctuate the campaign with escalating challenge, each requiring pattern recognition and creative use of the arm's capabilities. Environmental destruction during these encounters adds spectacle, with buildings crumbling and platforms collapsing under sustained fire. Product placement, particularly for Pepsi and Mountain Dew, appears throughout the environment in an oddly prominent manner that borders on unintentional comedy.

Technical issues surface intermittently: a single crash disrupted progression, and controller input drops caused missed inputs during critical sequences. Collectibles, scattered across levels, prove difficult to locate without guides, discouraging completionist play.

Overall, the bionic arm's traversal mechanics and the variety of combat options deliver an action-adventure whose creative movement sustains engagement, even if the targeting system and enemy density issues prevent the combat from reaching its potential.
