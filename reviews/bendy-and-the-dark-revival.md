Bendy And The Dark Revival drops Audrey into Joey Drew's cartoon studio through a mysterious elevator, trapping her in a world where ink-drenched corridors and lurking creatures replace the familiar animation cells. The Bioshock comparison surfaces immediately: resource management, ability upgrades, and environmental storytelling mirror that franchise's framework. From the opening moments, the art style communicates a world caught between whimsy and dread.

Between stealth sequences, the studio reveals itself through carefully lit corridors and environmental details that reward careful observation. Audrey's transformation into an ink-based being drives the narrative forward, while the ink demon demands constant awareness. Hiding spots, scattered throughout each area, provide brief respite before the next encounter.

The stealth system creates tension: lockers, barrels, and under-table hiding spots demand quick decisions about positioning and timing. When the ink demon appears, the absence of effective defensive options forces a flight response that heightens the stakes. Resource collection feeds into a crafting loop that upgrades weapons and abilities, though the combat itself lacks the mechanical depth to match the stealth's intensity.

The ink demon's behaviour proves inconsistent: it sometimes appears in corridors with no viable hiding spots, transforming tense exploration into unavoidable damage sequences. Keepers, faster-moving enemies that patrol key areas, compound this by demanding precise evasion that the controls do not always support. Platforming sections suffer from momentum issues where sprinting produces excessive speed while walking feels sluggish.

Save points tucked between combat zones require scrolling through menus, breaking the flow during intense sequences. The cartoon-horror aesthetic and atmospheric audio design maintain a consistent mood between these friction points.

Overall, the stealth-horror loop and cartoon-infiltrated atmosphere deliver a survival horror experience whose tension persists beyond its combat limitations, even if the ink demon's unpredictable appearances and absent defensive options prevent the mechanics from fully realising their potential.
