Kratos, now the God of War, is haunted by visions of a brother he believed dead. Learning Deimos is alive and imprisoned in the domain of death, Kratos embarks through Atlantis, Sparta, and the underworld to find him.

Combat revolves around the Blades of Chaos with the fire bane mechanic igniting blades for timed enhanced damage, alongside the Spear and Shield for ranged attacks. Three magic types expand tactical options: the Scourge of Erinys heals whilst damaging enemies, and the Horn of Boreas freezes targets.

Location diversity surpasses Chains of Olympus, with Atlantis, Sparta, the volcanic core, and the domain of death each offering distinct visual identities. Environmental detail pushes PSP hardware to its limits, with atmospheric lighting establishing scale that belies the platform's constraints.

The magic system's D-pad mapping proves awkward during combat, requiring thumb repositioning that disrupts flow. Excessive tutorials interrupt momentum by explaining mechanics discoverable through gameplay alone, though they never demand specific inputs.

Some enemy types attack from off-screen with significant range, creating unavoidable damage moments that test patience. The Spear and Shield, whilst available, rarely match the Blades' effectiveness, limiting their practical application.

Red orbs feel scarce compared to other series entries, with red chests providing negligible upgrades. Catchup mechanics ensure health and magic can be maxed through exploration, maintaining progression momentum despite the scarcity.

Overall, the emotionally resonant narrative about brotherhood and satisfying fire bane combat deliver a PSP entry that surpasses expectations. Awkward magic controls and excessive tutorials prevent the experience from matching its predecessor's polish.
