## The short version

- There are no accounts, sign-ins, comment sections, newsletters, or contact forms on this site.
- There is no advertising, no analytics, no tracking pixels, and no profiling. This site does not build a profile of you.
- This site sets no cookies of its own.
- The only data stored in your browser is a few small preferences, a local list of videos you have marked as watched, and a short-lived cache. None of it leaves your device.
- Your browser contacts a small number of third-party services to display content: GitHub (hosting), YouTube (video thumbnails), Google Sheets and two script libraries (the compatibility tables), and Twitch (the livestream player). Each is listed below with what it receives.
- If you email me, I receive what you send and use it to reply. Nothing more.

## Who runs this site

The Playability Report is a personal, non-commercial archive run by Rhys Wyrill. It is a static website, with no application server, database, or user account system behind it.

For anything in this policy, including any privacy request, contact **<a href="mailto:rhyswyrill@gmail.com">rhyswyrill@gmail.com</a>**.

This policy covers this website only. My videos on YouTube and my streams on Twitch are governed by those platforms' own policies, not this one.

## What this site does not collect

To be explicit, this site does not:

- ask for or store your name, email address, or any other identifier;
- run Google Analytics, or any other analytics or session-recording service;
- show advertising or use advertising or retargeting cookies;
- include social media tracking widgets, embedded "like" buttons, or share buttons that load anything before you click them;
- load its stylesheets, fonts, or main scripts from a third party; they are served from this site itself. The only third-party scripts are jQuery and PapaParse on the compatibility page, listed below;
- operate a mailing list or send marketing email.

## Information processed automatically

Like any website, this one has to be served to you. The hosting provider, GitHub Pages, receives the standard request information when your browser loads a page: your IP address, the page requested, your browser's user agent, the referring page, and a timestamp. GitHub uses this to deliver content and to protect against abuse, and retains it under its own policies. I do not have access to raw visitor logs.

GitHub also provides repository owners with aggregate traffic counts (page views and clones). Those figures are aggregated and are not linked to individual visitors.

## Cookies

This site sets no cookies. The theme includes an optional cookie-consent notice, but it is disabled because there is nothing to consent to: no analytics property is configured, so no analytics script is loaded either. If that ever changes, this page will be updated first, and a consent prompt will be added where the law requires one.

## What is stored in your browser

A few features would be annoying without remembering a little state. These use your browser's local storage, which stays on your device and is readable only by this site's pages. None of it is sent to me or to anyone else.

The site works fully without any of these; you lose only the remembered position and a small amount of extra loading. "Clear site data" (or "Clear cookies and site data") in your browser's privacy settings removes them.

Two details worth knowing:

- **No YouTube player is embedded on this site.** Video cards show a YouTube thumbnail and link out to YouTube in a new tab. Watching a video takes you to YouTube, where Google's policies apply.
- **The Twitch player is loaded only on the livestream page.** If the channel is offline, that page loads the most recent VOD player instead. If you would rather not contact Twitch at all, simply don't open that page; the rest of the site never requests anything from Twitch.

## Outbound links

Links to YouTube channels, Twitch, Steam, GitHub, PayPal, and social sharing destinations (X, Reddit, Facebook) are ordinary links. They contact nothing until you click them, and once you follow one, the destination's privacy policy applies. Donations through PayPal are handled entirely by PayPal; this site never sees your payment details.

## If you email me

If you send an email to the address above, I receive your email address, whatever name you sign with, and the contents of your message. I use it to reply and to keep a record of the conversation. I do not add you to any list, and I do not sell or share it. Emails are processed by Gmail, so Google handles them under its own policy. I keep correspondence only as long as it is useful, and you can ask me to delete it at any time.

## Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights over your personal data, including access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability, and the right to complain to a supervisory authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, your national authority). California residents have equivalent rights, and this site does not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined by the CCPA/CPRA.

In practice, this site holds almost no personal data of its own: browser storage stays on your device, and hosting logs are held by GitHub. A request about a log entry, a Google request, or a Twitch cookie is best directed to that provider, but email me and I will help where I can. A request about an email you sent me can be made directly to the address above.

There is no tracking here to opt out of, so nothing changes in response to "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control signals. Third-party services listed above apply their own rules.

## Children

This site is not directed at children and has no accounts or interactive features a child could sign up for. I do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If a child has emailed me, tell me and I will delete the correspondence.

## Changes to this policy

If what the site does with data changes, for example if analytics were ever added, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed before the change takes effect. There is no need to check back often; the policy describes a site that deliberately collects as little as possible, and it is not expected to change.
